# 06. 타 프로그램 비교 A (`program-comparison-a`)

> 2026-10-15 REF5 근거 평가 워크플로의 원자료다. 본문은 조사·검증 에이전트가 쓴 영어 원문 그대로다. 상태: confirmed(원문 확인), corrected(출처는 있으나 서지·수치를 고침, `검증 결과`를 쓸 것), unverifiable(확인 불가, 재확인 전 근거로 쓰지 말 것), misattributed(저자 귀속 오류).

## 조사 범위

Comparison with established programs, part A (submaximal / autoregulated / frequency-oriented): Tactical Barbell (Operator, Zulu, Fighter), Wendler 5/3/1 (original, Beginner, Forever Leader/Anchor, Krypteia), Pavel (Easy Strength, Grease the Groove, Power to the People), Dan John, Broz / Bulgarian / daily max, Sheiko, Tuchscherer RTS / Emerging Strategies. All compared with REF5 v1.4 at the level of principles.

### PCA-01 · corrected

**Pelland JC, Remmert JF, Robinson ZP, Hinson SR, Zourdos MC (2025 online / 2026 print). The Resistance Training Dose Response: Meta-Regressions Exploring the Effects of Weekly Volume and Frequency on Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength Gains. Sports Med 56(2):481-505. doi:10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w**

- meta-analysis · 2025 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/
- 조사 요약: Bayesian meta-regressions over 67 studies (2058 participants). Weekly volume had a ~100% posterior probability of a positive effect on strength, but with strong diminishing returns; strength reached a functional plateau at moderate volumes. Weekly frequency had a positive effect on strength (coefficient 3.27, CrI 2.74–3.84), also with diminishing returns, and a negligible effect on hypertrophy once volume was accounted for. Fractional sets counted, and even very low doses produced gains.
- 검증 결과: Verified from the abstract via Europe PMC: 67 studies, 2058 participants, multilevel Bayesian meta-regressions, with fractional set counting best supported. Volume had a 100% posterior probability of a positive effect on both hypertrophy and strength, with diminishing returns that were more pronounced for strength. Frequency had a 100% posterior probability of a positive effect on strength, also with diminishing returns, and a negligible effect on hypertrophy. Not verified (absent from the abstract, full text paywalled): the frequency coefficient 3.27 (CrI 2.74-3.84), a 'functional plateau at moderate volumes', and the claim that 'even very low doses produced gains'.
- 검증 메모: The author list is exactly 5 people (no 'et al.') and the title ends 'Strength Gains'. It was online December 2025, in print 2026;56(2). PubMed itself was blocked by reCAPTCHA, so the record was read through the Europe PMC API. Cite 'diminishing returns' rather than 'plateau' unless the full text is checked.
- REF5 관련성: This is the central evidence for REF5's design, which pairs high frequency with low volume. It supports squatting every session (§7, §9) and the ~10-set session ceiling, which avoids the athlete's nSuns failure. It also shows that the lowest-volume lifts at 2x/wk (PULL 3, DL 2, OHP 2 sets/wk, §19.3) sit on the steep part of the dose-response curve.
- 조사 당시 표기: Pelland JC, Remmert JF, Robinson ZP, Hinson SR, Zourdos MC, et al. (2025). The Resistance Training Dose Response: Meta-Regressions Exploring the Effects of Weekly Volume and Frequency on Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength Gain. Sports Medicine.

### PCA-02 · confirmed

**Currier BS, D'Souza AC, Singh MAF, ... Phillips SM (2026). American College of Sports Medicine Position Stand. Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance in Healthy Adults: An Overview of Reviews. Med Sci Sports Exerc 58(4):851-872.**

- position stand · 2026 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/
- 조사 요약: An overview of 137 systematic reviews covering more than 30,000 participants. Strength is enhanced by loads of at least 80% of 1RM, full range of motion, 2–3 sets, placing the exercise early in the session, and at least 2 sessions per week. Hypertrophy is enhanced by at least 10 sets per week. Training to failure and periodization model did not consistently change outcomes.
- 검증 결과: An overview of 137 systematic reviews covering more than 30,000 participants (search to October 2024). Strength gains were greater with loads of at least 80% 1RM, full ROM, 2-3 sets, performing the exercise at the start of the session, and at least 2 sessions/wk. Hypertrophy was enhanced by at least 10 sets/wk and by eccentric overload. Training to failure, periodization, equipment, set structure and some other variables had no consistent effect.
- 검증 메모: Full title confirmed (above). Published online 5 March 2026.
- REF5 관련성: Supports REF5's squat-first order (§7.2–7.3), its hard-squat intensity (H3 ≈ 82%, H2 ≈ 87% of e1RM), set termination short of failure (§11.1) and a frequency of 2x/wk or more. The ≥10 sets/wk hypertrophy benchmark puts a number on the hypertrophy cost of REF5's low volume (SQ 6, BP 5 sets/wk at 2x/wk).
- 조사 당시 표기: Currier BS, et al. (2026). American College of Sports Medicine Position Stand: resistance training prescription for healthy adults (title abbreviated). Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise 58(4):851–872.

### PCA-03 · confirmed

**Currier BS, Mcleod JC, Banfield L, ... D'Souza AC, ... Phillips SM (2023). Resistance training prescription for muscle strength and hypertrophy in healthy adults: a systematic review and Bayesian network meta-analysis. Br J Sports Med 57(18):1211-1220.**

- meta-analysis · 2023 · https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/57/18/1211
- 조사 요약: Network meta-analysis of 178 studies (n = 5097). Higher loads (above 80% of 1RM) maximized strength. The top-ranked prescription for strength was high-load, multi-set training 3x/wk: SMD 1.60 (95% CrI 1.38–1.82).
- 검증 결과: Strength network: 178 studies, n = 5097. Every prescription beat no exercise. The top-ranked prescription for strength was higher-load, multiset, 3x/wk (HM3): SMD 1.60 (95% CrI 1.38-1.82) versus control. Higher loads ranked highest for strength, and multiple sets ranked highest for hypertrophy (the top hypertrophy prescription was HM2, SMD 0.66).
- 검증 메모: The first and last authors are correct. The exact numeric cutoff for 'higher load' (>80% 1RM) was not visible in the abstract text retrieved.
- REF5 관련성: REF5's hard squat (H3/H2) sits above 80% e1RM at every exposure. Tactical Barbell Operator reaches that only in 2 of 6 weeks, and 5/3/1 Forever top sets reach only about 72–81% of 1RM. REF5's V (about 73% e1RM) and its volume slots fall below the threshold and act as frequency and practice work.
- 조사 당시 표기: Currier BS, D'Souza AC, Phillips SM, et al. (2023). Resistance training prescription for muscle strength and hypertrophy in healthy adults: a systematic review and Bayesian network meta-analysis. British Journal of Sports Medicine 57(18):1211–1220.

### PCA-04 · confirmed

**Grgic J, Schoenfeld BJ, Davies TB, Lazinica B, Krieger JW, Pedisic Z (2018). Effect of Resistance Training Frequency on Gains in Muscular Strength: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Sports Med 48(5):1207-1220.**

- meta-analysis · 2018 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29470825/
- 조사 요약: Strength effect sizes rose with sessions per week: 0.74, 0.82, 0.93 and 1.08 for 1, 2, 3 and 4+ sessions/wk. In volume-equated comparisons the frequency effect was not significant (p = 0.421). Frequency mattered for multi-joint exercises. Samples were mostly untrained.
- 검증 결과: 22 studies. Effect sizes were 0.74, 0.82, 0.93 and 1.08 for 1, 2, 3 and 4+ sessions/wk (overall p = 0.003). The volume-equated frequency effect was not significant (p = 0.421). The frequency effect was significant for multi-joint 1RM (p < 0.001) and upper-body strength (p = 0.004), but NOT for lower-body strength (p = 0.070) or for men (p = 0.190). The authors conclude that gains appear to be driven mainly by volume, and most participants were untrained.
- 검증 메모: The non-significant lower-body result is directly relevant to the squat and was omitted from the original finding.
- REF5 관련성: Explains why the athlete's original 5/3/1 (squat 1x/wk) and the Krypteia layout (one main lift per day, 2 days/wk) are poor fits. REF5's daily SQ exposure (§7, §9) captures the frequency benefit mostly through added weekly volume and practice.
- 조사 당시 표기: Grgic J, Schoenfeld BJ, Davies TB, Lazinica B, Krieger JW, Pedisic Z (2018). Effect of Resistance Training Frequency on Gains in Muscular Strength: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Sports Medicine 48(5):1207–1220.

### PCA-05 · confirmed

**Colquhoun RJ, Gai CM, Aguilar D, Bove D, Dolan J, Vargas A, Couvillion K, Jenkins NDM, Campbell BI (2018). Training Volume, Not Frequency, Indicative of Maximal Strength Adaptations to Resistance Training. J Strength Cond Res 32(5):1207-1213.**

- RCT · 2018 · https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/Fulltext/2018/05000/Training_Volume,_Not_Frequency,_Indicative_of.3.aspx
- 조사 요약: Trained men did volume-equated training at 3x/wk or 6x/wk for 6 weeks. Squat gains were +16.8 kg and +16.7 kg, with no difference between groups.
- 검증 결과: Resistance-trained men trained 3x/wk or 6x/wk for 6 weeks with volume and intensity matched (28 of 43 completed: 16 vs 12). Both groups increased squat 1RM, with no group x time interaction (p = 0.845).
- 검증 메모: The kg values (+16.8 vs +16.7) are in Table 2, which could not be retrieved, so the numbers are unverified. The direction of the finding is confirmed.
- REF5 관련성: At fixed weekly volume, more than about 3 heavy exposures per week adds little. This makes REF5's cap of fewer than 2 hard SQ starts per 168 h (§9) a low-cost restriction rather than a large loss. It also argues against Broz-style daily training for this athlete.
- 조사 당시 표기: Colquhoun RJ, Gai CM, Aguilar D, et al. (2018). Training Volume, Not Frequency, Indicative of Maximal Strength Adaptations to Resistance Training. Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research 32(5):1207–1213.

### PCA-06 · confirmed

**Ralston GW, Kilgore L, Wyatt FB, Baker JS (2017). The Effect of Weekly Set Volume on Strength Gain: A Meta-Analysis. Sports Med 47(12):2585-2601.**

- meta-analysis · 2017 · https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-017-0762-7
- 조사 요약: High weekly set volume beat low weekly set volume for strength: ES 0.18 (95% CI 0.06–0.30); for multi-joint exercises ES 0.18; medium versus low ES 0.15. The low-volume category is roughly 5 or fewer weekly sets per exercise, as summarized in later reviews.
- 검증 결과: 9 studies, 61 treatment groups. High vs low weekly sets: ES 0.18 (95% CI 0.06-0.30). Medium vs low: ES 0.15 (0.01-0.30). Multi-joint high vs low: ES 0.18 (0.01-0.34). Exercise-specific 1RM high vs low was not significant: ES 0.14 (-0.01 to 0.29), p = 0.06. Categories per exercise per week: low ≤5 sets, medium 5-9, high ≥10. The authors suggest medium or high volume for well-trained lifters.
- 검증 메모: The category cutoffs come from the paper itself, not only from 'later reviews'. At 6 sets/wk, REF5's SQ falls in the medium category.
- REF5 관련성: At 2x/wk, REF5 gives PULL 3, DL 2 and OHP 2 direct sets per week, which falls in the low-volume category, and spec §22.4 item 1 already flags this. SQ at 2x/wk (6 sets/wk) sits just above the cutoff.
- 조사 당시 표기: Ralston GW, Kilgore L, Wyatt FB, Baker JS (2017). The Effect of Weekly Set Volume on Strength Gain: A Meta-Analysis. Sports Medicine 47(12):2585–2601.

### PCA-07 · confirmed

**Androulakis-Korakakis P, Fisher JP, Steele J (2020). The Minimum Effective Training Dose Required to Increase 1RM Strength in Resistance-Trained Men: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Sports Med 50(4):751-765.**

- meta-analysis · 2020 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31797219/
- 조사 요약: In trained men, a single set of 6–12 reps at 70–85% of 1RM done 1–3x/wk produced significant but suboptimal 1RM gains: squat +17.48 kg and bench press +8.25 kg over the study periods.
- 검증 결과: 6 studies were included and 5 meta-analysed. A single set done 1-3x/wk produced significant 1RM gains: overall +12.09 kg (8.16-16.03), squat +17.48 kg (8.51-26.46), bench +8.25 kg (0.68-15.83). The practical conclusion is 1 set of 6-12 reps at about 70-85% 1RM, 2-3x/wk, taken to volitional or momentary failure, for 8-12 weeks: suboptimal but significant gains in trained men. Evidence for the deadlift is less clear.
- 검증 메모: The authors' practical dose is 2-3x/wk and to failure. REF5's DL and OHP (2 non-failure sets/wk) sit below that dose, and the evidence for the deadlift is weakest.
- REF5 관련성: Even REF5's minimal 2x/wk dose for DL and OHP is enough to keep strength rising slowly or at least maintain it, consistent with their secondary role (§13.3). Note that this dose was taken to failure, unlike REF5.
- 조사 당시 표기: Androulakis-Korakakis P, Fisher JP, Steele J (2020). The Minimum Effective Training Dose Required to Increase 1RM Strength in Resistance-Trained Men: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Sports Medicine 50(4):751–765.

### PCA-08 · confirmed

**Robinson ZP, Pelland JC, Remmert JF, Refalo MC, Jukic I, Steele J, Zourdos MC (2024). Exploring the Dose-Response Relationship Between Estimated Resistance Training Proximity to Failure, Strength Gain, and Muscle Hypertrophy: A Series of Meta-Regressions. Sports Med 54(9):2209-2231.**

- meta-analysis · 2024 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38970765/
- 조사 요약: Estimated repetitions in reserve had a negligible relationship with strength gain. Hypertrophy increased as sets were taken closer to failure.
- 검증 결과: For strength, the intervals around the estimated-RIR slope included zero (a negligible relationship). For hypertrophy, gains increased as sets were taken closer to failure. It also weakens the athlete's explanation that Tactical Barbell Operator failed because of insufficient proximity. Training far from failure costs hypertrophy, not strength.

### PCA-09 · confirmed

**Jukic I, Pérez Castilla A, García Ramos A, Van Hooren B, McGuigan MR, Helms ER (2023; online 30 Sep 2022). The Acute and Chronic Effects of Implementing Velocity Loss Thresholds During Resistance Training. Sports Med 53:177-214.**

### PCA-10 · confirmed

**Pareja-Blanco F, Rodríguez-Rosell D, Sánchez-Medina L, et al. (2017). Effects of velocity loss during resistance training on athletic performance, strength gains and muscle adaptations. Scand J Med Sci Sports 27(7):724-735.**

- RCT · 2017 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27038416/
- 조사 요약: Squat training to 20% velocity loss (VL20) and to 40% velocity loss (VL40) gave similar 1RM gains, with VL20 doing about 40% fewer reps. Countermovement jump improved 9.5% with VL20 and 3.5% with VL40. VL40 produced more hypertrophy and a loss of type IIX fibers.
- 검증 결과: 8 weeks of squat training in 22 young men (VL20 n = 12, VL40 n = 10). Squat strength gains were similar. CMJ improved 9.5% with VL20 vs 3.5% with VL40, even though VL20 did 40% fewer reps. VL40 produced greater hypertrophy of the vastus lateralis and intermedius and reduced MHC IIX, which VL20 preserved.
- 검증 메모: The abstract gives no kg values for the 1RM gains.
- REF5 관련성: Mechanistic RCT support for REF5's low-fatigue set termination in the squat (§11.1). The pattern of equal strength from less fatigue mirrors the Easy Strength, Sheiko and Tactical Barbell ethos.
- 조사 당시 표기: Pareja-Blanco F, Rodriguez-Rosell D, Sanchez-Medina L, et al. (2017). Effects of velocity loss during resistance training on athletic performance, strength gains and muscle adaptations. Scandinavian Journal of Medicine & Science in Sports 27(7):724–735.

### PCA-11 · confirmed

**Hickmott LM, Chilibeck PD, Shaw KA, Butcher SJ (2022). The Effect of Load and Volume Autoregulation on Muscular Strength and Hypertrophy: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Sports Med Open 8:9.**

- meta-analysis · 2022 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8762534/
- 조사 요약: Autoregulated versus standardized load gave a non-significant 1RM difference: MD +2.07 kg (95% CI −0.32 to 4.46), SMD 0.21. Velocity loss of 25% or less, versus more than 25%, gave greater 1RM (MD 2.32 kg, SMD 0.23) but less hypertrophy (SMD 0.28 favoring higher velocity loss). The strength benefit of low velocity loss appeared mainly when protocols included additional training outside the intervention (SMD 0.62).
- 검증 결과: 15 studies in resistance-trained people. Autoregulated vs standardized load for 1RM: MD 2.07 kg (95% CI -0.32 to 4.46), p = 0.09, SMD 0.21 (not significant). Velocity loss ≤25% vs >25% for 1RM: MD 2.32 kg (0.33-4.31), SMD 0.23, favouring ≤25%. For CSA, ≤25% gave less hypertrophy: MD 0.61 cm², SMD 0.28. These comparisons hold when sets and relative intensity are equated.
- 검증 메모: The subgroup claim that the low-VL strength benefit appeared mainly with additional outside training (SMD 0.62) is NOT in the abstract and could not be verified (PMC was blocked by reCAPTCHA; the abstract was read via Europe PMC).
- REF5 관련성: Load autoregulation like REF5's (§13–§15) yields at most a small strength benefit over fixed loading. REF5's main value is preventing training-max drift, not boosting gains. Low-fatigue sets help most when other activity (for this athlete, climbing) competes for recovery.
- 조사 당시 표기: Hickmott LM, Chilibeck PD, Shaw KA, Butcher SJ (2022). The Effect of Load and Volume Autoregulation on Muscular Strength and Hypertrophy: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Sports Medicine – Open 8:9.

### PCA-12 · corrected

- REF5 관련성: Weak-to-moderate support for having a closed-loop progression at all (§13–§14), compared with the athlete's open-loop percentage-based experience on Tactical Barbell Operator.
- 조사 당시 표기: Zhang X, Li H, Bi S, Luo Y, Cao Y, Zhang G (2021). Auto-Regulation Method vs. Fixed-Loading Method in Maximum Strength Training for Athletes: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Frontiers in Physiology 12:651112.

### PCA-15 · confirmed

**Helms ER, Byrnes RK, Cooke DM, et al. (2018). RPE vs. Percentage 1RM Loading in Periodized Programs Matched for Sets and Repetitions. Front Physiol 9:247.**

- RCT · 2018 · https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2018.00247/full
- 조사 요약: Squat improved +17.1 kg with RPE-based loading and +13.9 kg with percentage-based loading: ES 0.50, with a 79% likelihood of an RPE advantage. Average session RPE in the RPE group was about 7.2–7.3, and rest periods were long.

### PCA-16 · confirmed

**Graham T, Cleather DJ (2021). Autoregulation by 'Repetitions in Reserve' Leads to Greater Improvements in Strength Over a 12-Week Training Program Than Fixed Loading. J Strength Cond Res 35(9):2451-2456.**

## REF5 함의: 검증 후 유지

- §11.1 stopping short of failure is supported for strength by confirmed meta-analytic and RCT evidence: Robinson 2024 (negligible RIR-strength relationship), Jukic 2023 (VL threshold did not affect strength), and Pareja-Blanco 2017 (VL20 roughly equal to VL40 for squat strength with 40% fewer reps). The hypertrophy cost is confirmed by Robinson 2024, Jukic 2023 (higher VL favours hypertrophy), Hickmott 2022 (SMD 0.28 favouring >25% VL) and Pareja-Blanco 2017. The shared submaximal ethos is confirmed for Tactical Barbell ('below the threshold of fatigue and failure') and Sheiko/Russian ('rarely ever miss a rep').
- §9 frequency (squat in every session, at least 2 hard exposures where possible) is supported: Pelland 2025 found a 100% posterior probability that frequency helps strength, with diminishing returns; Grgic 2018 effect sizes were 0.74 to 1.08; ACSM 2026 recommends at least 2 sessions/wk. In Colquhoun 2018, 3x vs 6x/wk at matched volume showed no squat difference (p = 0.845), so the 2-hard-per-168 h cap is cheap. Caveat: Grgic's frequency effect was non-significant when volume was equated and for lower-body strength (p = 0.070), so the benefit runs mainly through volume.
- Hard-squat intensity (H3 about 82%, H2 about 87%) sits in the range ACSM 2026 (at least 80% 1RM) and Currier 2023 (higher load ranked first for strength; HM3 SMD 1.60) associate with greater strength. Liftosaur confirms that Tactical Barbell Operator reaches 80% 1RM or more only in weeks 3 (81%) and 6 (85%). V (about 73%) should be read as practice and volume.
- §7.2-7.3 order (SQ first) is supported by ACSM 2026 ('performing the exercise at the start of sessions').
- No scheduled deload; the evidence is not strong enough even for a §26-style proposal. Coleman 2024 (cessation week: posterior probabilities 0.851 and 0.924 favouring continuous training) and Rogerson 2024 ('clear lack of empirical research') are confirmed, and the Delphi (Bell 2023, Sports Med Open 9:87) is consensus only. This holds with the caveats listed under weakened.
- §13.1 window length: the research is silent on this. Graham & Cleather 2021 (confirmed: +10.8% vs +7.1% back squat, with mean intensity 83.6% vs 80.4%) suggests that faster load tracking can matter. Wendler 2024 (confirmed: 'When there is a doubt, there is no doubt'; stay one cycle longer at a lighter TM) supports patience. The conclusion that a shorter window is at most a later single §26 candidate, not a current recommendation, still holds.
- §14/§15 closed loop corresponds to the coaching consensus: 5/3/1's 7th-week TM test (fewer than 3 reps at 100% TM lowers the TM; LiftVault, confirmed), Wendler's account post ('if you can't hit your TM for 5 strong reps, your TM is too high'), and Tactical Barbell's lack of AMRAP or autoregulation plus a 6-12-week retest (Liftosaur and LiftVault, confirmed). Research is silent on REF5's thresholds.
- Autoregulation in general probably offers only small strength benefits: Hickmott 2022 MD +2.07 kg, not significant, SMD 0.21; Huang 2025 found no moderate or large squat differences between methods. REF5's value lies mainly in preventing TM drift and keeping input low.
- §19.3 low weekly volume for PULL, DL and OHP at 2x/wk falls in Ralston 2017's low category (5 or fewer sets/exercise/wk, confirmed), and Pelland 2025 confirms diminishing returns. That makes a rate-of-progress concern for these lifts reasonable. Androulakis-Korakakis 2020 (confirmed) shows minimal doses still produce gains, though with failure sets at 2-3x/wk.
- The hypertrophy cost of C1 and low volume is a described cost: ACSM 2026 (at least 10 sets/wk) and Robinson 2024 (proximity) are confirmed. Pelland 2025 confirms that frequency has a negligible effect on hypertrophy.
- C9 cost: the compared programs all have a maximal reality check that REF5 lacks. 5/3/1's 7th-week AMRAP at 100% TM and Tactical Barbell's 6-12-week retest are confirmed.
- Perceived velocity loss is usable without devices, with about 1 rep of error (Dello Iacono 2023, confirmed). Halperin 2022 (confirmed) shows perceptual estimates of proximity are noisy, which supports the user's exclusion of RIR. A single-exposure judgment therefore carries noise about the size of the HOLD/FAIL boundary, and multi-exposure windows dampen it.
- nSuns and 5x5 verdict: Moesgaard 2022 (confirmed) found undulating better than linear in trained participants (ES 0.61, CI 0.00-1.22, borderline), consistent with REF5's H3/H2/V undulation.
- Sheiko verdict: the JTS sources (confirmed) support about 1000 lifts/month at 67-69% for advanced lifters, about 70% average intensity, rare misses, and only about 2 days at 100% per roughly 14-week cycle. This is a high-volume, moderate-intensity route opposite to REF5's.
- Broz/Bulgarian verdict (only the frequency principle transfers): Broz's daily maxing up to 13 sessions/wk and 'How you feel is a lie' are confirmed (Contreras 2011). Zourdos 2016 is confirmed as a 3-person case series (peak gains of +5.8%, +10.8% and +9.5%), and Androulakis-Korakakis 2018 is confirmed as a fragile pilot in which 3 of 5 lowered their total.
- 5/3/1 verdict: original and Krypteia layouts contradict the frequency evidence. This is strengthened, because Krypteia trains each main lift about once every 2 weeks. Wendler's 'err on the side of too light' and 'earn the right to add weight' (Beginner page, confirmed) support REF5's conservative start.
- RTS/Emerging Strategies as a conceptual relative that handles schedule changes: the TRAC-R description (schedule changes, overshot RPEs, variable recovery) is confirmed, as is the dependence of fatigue-percent methods on RPE accuracy. This supports calling REF5 a low-input, low-resolution alternative.

## REF5 함의: 검증에서 약화

- §11.1 and the RTS verdict: the claim that 'RTS defines RPE 8 as no longer being able to keep fast bar speed', and so equals REF5's slowdown stop, is NOT supported. The cited Fatigue Percents page gives no bar-speed definition of RPE 8 and no 3/5/7% tiers. The REF5-RTS equivalence should be framed as an analogy, not a documented definition.
- §11.1-§11.3 'errors will be lenient (false PASS)': this rests on unverified Sindiani 2020 numbers (the 4.2x squat underestimation could not be checked). Dello Iacono 2023 found NO accuracy difference between 20% and 40% VL thresholds. Dello Stritto 2025 found the least reliable condition was 60% 1RM with 20% VL, not small VL at heavy loads, and did not test whether practice improves accuracy. The direction of bias is therefore uncertain. Expect about 1 rep of noise (Dello Iacono 2023), not a predictable lenient bias. A video-calibration habit remains a reasonable non-protocol suggestion, but its justification is weaker.
- Session queue and the Tactical Barbell Operator verdict: 'TB author wouldn't recommend 2 days/wk' is a misreading. ## REF5 함의: 조사 단계 원안

- §11.1 set termination (stop at the first obvious slowdown, never to failure). SUPPORTED for strength by direct evidence: Robinson 2024 found a negligible link between proximity to failure and strength gain; Jukic 2023 found velocity-loss threshold did not affect strength; Pareja-Blanco 2017 found VL20 equal to VL40 for squat 1RM with 40% fewer reps. Every submaximal program in this comparison shares the principle: Tactical Barbell ('below the threshold of fatigue and failure'), Pavel/Dan John ('never miss'), Sheiko ('rarely miss'), and RTS (RPE 8 = 'can't keep fast bar speed'). The cost is hypertrophy, which rises with proximity (Robinson 2024; Hickmott 2022 SMD 0.28; Pareja-Blanco 2017). That is a described cost of C1/C3, not a reason to change the rule.
- §11.1–§11.3 binary slowdown judgment, with ambiguous slowdowns not recorded. The research is SILENT on REF5's binary rule itself, and indirect evidence predicts a lenient bias. Perceived velocity loss errs by about 1 rep (Dello Iacono 2023). In the squat, velocity changes are 4.2x more often underestimated (Sindiani 2020). Perception is least reliable at small velocity losses (Dello Stritto 2025), and RIR is underpredicted by about 0.95 reps (Halperin 2022). Most misjudgments in SQ will therefore be false PASSes, not false FAILs. Within the fixed criteria (FAIL 0, HOLD ≤1, volume veto unchanged), the mitigation is operational: perception improves with practice (Sindiani 2020; Dello Stritto 2025), so the athlete could occasionally video the last H2 set to calibrate. This is not a protocol change and needs no device. RTS carries the same perceptual-skill dependency through RPE.
- §9 SQ hard-day rules (≥48 h since the last hard start, fewer than 2 hard starts per 168 h, otherwise V) and squatting in every normal and micro session. SUPPORTED. Frequency helps strength with diminishing returns (Pelland 2025; Grgic 2018 effect sizes 0.74 to 1.08 for 1 to 4+ sessions/wk), and ACSM 2026 recommends at least 2 sessions/wk. This is the direct structural fix for the athlete's original 5/3/1 failure (squat 1x/wk) and for Krypteia, which trains each main lift about once a week across 2 days. The 2-hard-per-week cap is cheap: in Colquhoun 2018, 6x/wk equalled 3x/wk at equal volume (+16.7 vs +16.8 kg squat). The exact 48 h and 168 h boundaries are untested design values (§22.3), so the evidence is SILENT on them. V (3x5 at about 73% e1RM) is below the ≥80% strength-load range in ACSM 2026 and Currier 2023, so it should be read as practice and volume, not as a maximal-strength stimulus.
- Hard-squat intensity (H3 85 kg ≈ 82.5% and H2 90 kg ≈ 87% of a ~103 kg e1RM). SUPPORTED by Currier 2023 and ACSM 2026, which favor loads of 80% or more for strength. Compared with the programs the athlete failed on, REF5 sits higher. Tactical Barbell Operator (TM 90%) reaches 80% of 1RM or more only in weeks 3 and 6 (81% and 85.5%); its other weeks run 63–76.5%. 5/3/1 Forever top sets (TM 85%) run about 72–81%, and TB Fighter about 67–81%. Bulgarian/Broz (daily maxes) and Sheiko (about 70% average with very high volume) bracket REF5 from above and below.
- §13.1 SQ window (6 hard exposures, then +2.5 kg if FAIL 0, HOLD ≤1 and no V veto). The evidence is SILENT on the window length (§22.3). At current loads the rule rarely discriminates: 3x3 at about 82% leaves roughly 4–5 reps in reserve (Nuzzo 2024: about 8–9 reps possible at 80%). That fits the record of every window ending INCREASE, so in practice REF5 currently behaves as a slow fixed progression of about +3 kg per month. This lies between Tactical Barbell (about +4.5 kg per 6-week block for lower body) and original 5/3/1 (+4.5 kg per 3–4-week cycle). The all-INCREASE record is not evidence of failure. Graham & Cleather 2021 (RIR-tracked loads gave +10.8% vs +7.1% squat at a higher mean intensity, 83.6% vs 80.4%) and the APRE literature suggest faster load tracking can add strength, but Robinson 2024 says proximity itself barely matters, and Wendler's coaching ('err light, earn the right') argues for patience. Any change would be a single §26-style candidate, such as a shorter SQ window, conditional on several consecutive windows with zero HOLD and zero FAIL. The FAIL 0 / HOLD ≤1 / veto criteria would stay as they are. The evidence is not strong enough to recommend this now.
- §14 decrease (−2.5 kg after 2 consecutive FAILs in the same stream) and §15 stagnation (micro, reassessment, then −2.5 kg). These match the coaching consensus of every closed-loop program in the comparison: Wendler's resets and 'a higher TM does not equal more strength', the 7th-week TM test (fewer than 3 reps at 100% TM lowers the TM), and Tactical Barbell's advice to retest every 6–12 weeks. Research is SILENT on the specific thresholds (§22.3). This closed loop is exactly what Tactical Barbell Operator lacks: it has no AMRAP and no built-in autoregulation, and its forced progression is how the athlete's TM drifted unnoticed. Known gap: §15 cannot tell overload from underload (§22.4 item 3). RTS-style fatigue measures or e1RM tracking would separate them but require RPE, which the user's principles exclude. No change is recommended.
- Autoregulation in general (§11 within-session, §8/§9 between-session, §13–§15 program-level, in the Greig 2020 framework). The meta-analytic evidence suggests small benefits at most: Hickmott 2022 found autoregulated load +2.07 kg (not significant, SMD 0.21); Zhang 2021 found autoregulation better than fixed loading in athletes (8 small studies); Huang 2025 ranked APRE above RPE above VBT above percentage-based loading for the squat, with no moderate or large differences and low certainty. Direct evidence is SILENT on REF5's rep-deficit variant (§22.2). REF5's main benefit should be expected from preventing TM drift and keeping input low, not from a larger gain rate.
- No scheduled deload; the MICRO valve (§8) acts as a reactive deload (§22.2). SUPPORTED by the best available RCT: in Coleman 2024, one week of complete cessation mid-program likely worsened lower-body strength (posterior probability 0.851 for 1RM, 0.924 isometric) compared with continuous training. Scheduled-deload practice rests on consensus and surveys (Bell 2023 Delphi: every 4–6 weeks for about 7 days, intended to manage fatigue, not boost performance; Rogerson 2024: every 5.6 ± 2.3 weeks, 'clear lack of empirical research'). Programs with built-in unloading (5/3/1's 7th week, Perryman Bulgarian every 2–4 weeks, Sheiko light weeks) carry much higher intensity or volume than REF5. Evidence is not strong enough to raise even a §26-style deload proposal.
- §19.4 gap-return guidance (1 week: no action; 1–2 weeks: resume; 2–4 weeks: micro first; 4–8 weeks: start 2.5–5 kg lower) and §19.2 (no make-up sessions). SUPPORTED by detraining data: in Bosquet 2013, strength loss became significant only from about the third week of cessation (overall SMD −0.46). Spiering 2021 found strength maintained for up to 32 weeks on 1 session and 1 set per week at maintained intensity. The athlete's 8-day gap correctly needed no adjustment. The table boundaries remain conservative operating values, though they agree with this time course.
- §8 objective MICRO triggers (two consecutive program days, three normal starts in the prior 6 days, multi-lift FAIL token) rather than feel-based volume. SUPPORTED indirectly. In Bartolomei 2024, volume autoregulated by perceived recovery gave 17.4% more volume and no added strength, suggesting people overestimate their recovery. Bonilla 2025 found adherence fell when trainees chose sessions freely. The Broz camp's 'How you feel is a lie' is the same idea from coaching opinion. The exact trigger values are untested (§22.3), so the evidence is SILENT on the numbers.
- Session queue and engine-chosen sessions (§7, §8, §12; no calendar weeks). There is no direct trial, so the evidence is SILENT. Indirect evidence favors it: flexible daily undulating periodization was non-inferior to fixed DUP in trained men (Colquhoun 2017: +15.6 vs +18.0 kg squat, not significant); undulating beat linear periodization in trained lifters (Moesgaard 2022, ES 0.61); and free-choice flexibility can hurt adherence (Bonilla 2025), which REF5 avoids by having the engine decide. Among the compared programs, Tactical Barbell (author 'wouldn't recommend' 2 days/wk), 5/3/1, Sheiko, Easy Strength (5 days/wk) and Broz (daily) all assume a regular calendar. Only RTS's TRAC-R handles missed sessions, and it needs RPE. REF5 fits the athlete's irregular schedule (C4) best at the lowest input burden.
- §19.3 weekly sets at 2x/wk (SQ 6, BP 5, PULL 3 + OAP 2 pairs, DL 2, OHP 2). For SQ and BP the volume is SUPPORTED as adequate for strength: Pelland 2025 shows strong diminishing returns, and the low ceiling avoids the athlete's nSuns failure from too much volume and Sheiko-scale lift counts. For PULL, DL and OHP at 2x/wk the evidence leans CONTRADICTING sufficiency for continued progress: they fall in Ralston 2017's low-volume range and on the steep part of Pelland's curve, which spec §22.4 item 1 already acknowledges. For maintenance and slow gain the dose is adequate (Androulakis-Korakakis 2020; Spiering 2021). Following the user's rule to read low success first as insufficient stimulus or recovery, the first lever is reaching 3x/wk through scheduling (§19.4), which needs no protocol change. The only protocol lever, done one change at a time, is §23.1 step 3 (volume slot +1 set) through §26.
- Hypertrophy cost of C1 (no accessories) and low volume during a cut toward about 70 kg. The evidence is a described cost, not grounds for a recommendation. ACSM 2026 ties hypertrophy to 10 or more sets per week and Robinson 2024 to proximity to failure; REF5 is below on both counts. Pelland 2025 found frequency adds little to hypertrophy. The long-horizon relative-strength goal will at some point depend on muscle mass, so REF5 should be expected to deliver mainly neural, skill and maintenance gains during the deficit. No accessories are proposed.
- C9 cost (no 1RM test, no AMRAP; §22.4 item 8). Every compared program has some reality check that REF5 lacks: 5/3/1 has AMRAPs and the 7th-week TM test, Tactical Barbell retests every 6–12 weeks, Sheiko has pre-competition max checks, Broz maxes daily, and RTS uses top-set RPE e1RM. REF5 is the only one with no direct maximal signal, so its e1RM/BW KPI cannot be verified from the control REF. Mattocks 2017 (in untrained men) suggests near-maximal practice carries specific value that REF5 forgoes, since its heaviest regular exposure is H2 at about 87%. How large this cost is for trained lifters is not established. This is a described cost; no change to C9 is proposed.
- §7.2–7.3 exercise order (SQ first, focus lift second). SUPPORTED: ACSM 2026 lists placement early in the session as a strength enhancer, and every compared program puts the main lift first.
- §7.5 OAP slot (3rd in the BP-focus session, about 1–1.5 exposures/wk, never in micro). The evidence is SILENT for one-arm pull-up skill. Coaching opinion (Pavel's GTG and Naked Warrior: frequent, fresh, at or below about 50% of max, 'practice, not a workout'; Easy Strength at 5 days/wk) favors a more frequent and fresher pattern than REF5's. However, the athlete's elbow and climbing load (§19.5) argue against high-frequency single-arm pulling, and GTG's support is anecdotal. Because BP focus barely overlaps with the pulling muscles, coming after BP likely costs little. No change is supported now. If §23 oscillation triggers fire, the first single-element §26 candidate would be OAP placement after SQ but before BP focus, keeping SQ first.
- §19.1 rest periods (240 s for focus work, 150 s for volume, 180 s for DL). SUPPORTED. Helms 2018 used long rests in trained lifters, and the trial spec §22.1 already cites favours rests over 2 minutes. Coaching sources (Power to the People, Sheiko) also prescribe several minutes.
- Program verdict, Tactical Barbell Operator (athlete FAILED). It is better than REF5 at a fixed 3x/wk squat and bench frequency and has almost no state to track. It is worse for this athlete because its progression is open-loop with no AMRAP and no autoregulation, so the TM can drift unnoticed, which is what happened. Four of its six weeks are also below 80% of 1RM, and it needs fixed days 1/3/5 (the author advises against 2/wk). Evidence (Robinson 2024) does not support 'insufficient proximity' as the main cause of the failure for strength; open-loop drift, mostly sub-80% loading and schedule mismatch are more plausible. REF5's §13–§15 closed loop and §9 queue address all three.
- Program verdict, Tactical Barbell Zulu and Fighter. Fighter (2 days/wk, all lifts, 3–5 sets at about 67–81% of 1RM) is the closest 2x/wk analogue to REF5. It trains pull-ups at both sessions, whereas REF5 at 2x/wk gives about one PULL-focus exposure per week. It shares Operator's open-loop weakness. Zulu's A/B split (each lift 2x/wk over 4 days) assumes a regular calendar. REF5's own spec §22.4 notes that the 2x/wk regime is its weak spot for PULL.
- Program verdict, Wendler 5/3/1. The original version (squat 1x/wk; athlete FAILED) CONTRADICTS the frequency evidence (Grgic 2018; Pelland 2025), and Krypteia repeats that layout. 5/3/1 for Beginners and Forever Leader/Anchor fix the frequency problem and add what REF5 deliberately omits: AMRAP feedback and a 7th-week TM test. Wendler's philosophy ('err on the side of too light', earn the right to add weight, stay lighter one cycle longer after a reset) directly supports REF5's conservative start, its clean-window rule (§13) and its reassessment step (§15). In principle, REF5 is 5/3/1 Beginner with the AMRAP replaced by binary rep-quality judgment, slightly higher intensity, and lower volume.
- Program verdicts, nSuns and 5x5 linear progression (athlete FAILED both). nSuns failed on volume, which is consistent with Pelland 2025's diminishing returns; REF5's 10-set session ceiling and 4-set micro avoid it. The 5x5 linear program stalled because it adds load every session with no slowdown or fatigue rule. REF5 replaces per-session increments with windowed judgment (§13) and undulation (§9), which Moesgaard 2022 supports (undulating better than linear in trained lifters, ES 0.61).
- Program verdict, Pavel (Easy Strength, Power to the People, Grease the Groove) and Dan John. Same ethos as REF5: few lifts, never miss, about 10 reps per lift, add weight when it feels light. REF5 formalizes 'feels light' as PASS windows and adds a downward rule (§14), which Easy Strength and Power to the People lack (their cycling is open-loop). They are better at very low fatigue and high practice frequency. They are worse for this athlete because they require 5 days/wk (incompatible with C4), and the GTG and Easy Strength evidence is coaching opinion only.
- Program verdict, Broz 'squat every day', Bulgarian and daily-max methods. Highest specificity, but incompatible with C9 (daily maxing), C4 (near-daily training) and the athlete's limited recovery. The research base is a 3-person case series (Zourdos 2016: +5.8–10.8% after an initial 2–4% decline) and a pilot in which 3 of 5 lifters lowered their total (Androulakis-Korakakis 2018). Even its advocates prescribe unloading every 2–4 weeks and reserve it for high-work-capacity advanced lifters. Only the frequency principle transfers, and REF5 already uses it.
- Program verdict, Sheiko. Shares REF5's submaximal, rarely-miss, no in-training-max principles. Its route is the opposite: about 70% average intensity with very high lift counts (about 1000 lifts per month for advanced lifters). It also requires an accurate 1RM for its percentages and a regular schedule. Given the athlete's nSuns volume intolerance and Pelland's strength plateau, REF5's low-volume, high-intensity route is the better match; Sheiko would mean a structural change, not a refinement.
- Program verdict, Tuchscherer RTS and Emerging Strategies. This is REF5's closest conceptual relative. RTS defines RPE 8 as no longer being able to keep fast bar speed, which matches REF5's slowdown stop (§11.1). Its fatigue percents dose volume within the session, and TRAC-R handles missed sessions and schedule changes. It is better in resolution: it can tell overload from underload, the gap in §22.4 item 3. It is worse given the user's principles because it needs RPE skill (C10), more input, and lifters can inflate or deflate RPE. REF5 is effectively a binary, low-input RTS, and that trade-off is a legitimate design choice rather than an error.

## 불확실성

- No study tests REF5's specific mechanisms: binary obvious-slowdown judgment, 6- and 4-exposure windows, the HOLD allowance and volume veto, 2-consecutive-FAIL decrease, the session queue, or the 48 h / 168 h hard-day boundaries. Every judgment on these is extrapolated from broader evidence (consistent with spec §22.3).
- The coaching programs compared here (Tactical Barbell, 5/3/1 variants, Easy Strength, GTG, Power to the People, Broz, Sheiko, RTS) have essentially no controlled trials of their own. Statements about them are coaching opinion or derived from program documents, and several sources (Liftosaur, Lift Vault, Black Iron Beast, forum posts) are secondary summaries.
- The perceived-velocity-loss studies mostly used bench press, Smith machines or small samples, and loads and rep counts unlike 3x3 at 82–87%. How well their error estimates (about 1 rep; squat changes underestimated) apply to REF5's barbell squat judgment is uncertain.
- The athlete's current 1RM is unknown. The ~103 kg e1RM comes from 92.5 kg x3 at RPE 9–10 and may have drifted. The estimated reps in reserve at H3/H2 (about 4–5 at 82%) rely on population averages (Nuzzo 2024) that vary widely between people.
- Most of the evidence comes from young men in 6–12-week interventions. Long-horizon (multi-year) relative-strength outcomes under an energy deficit are essentially unstudied, so the hypertrophy cost of REF5's low volume during a cut cannot be quantified for this athlete.
- Bodyweight is not actually measured (the logged 75 kg is probably a default), so relative-strength KPIs and PULL total load are uncertain regardless of the program.
- The athlete's own diagnosis of the Tactical Barbell Operator failure ('insufficient proximity') conflicts with Robinson 2024 for strength. Without logs from that period it cannot be settled whether TM drift, sub-80% loading, scheduling or recovery was the main cause.
- Autoregulation meta-analyses (Hickmott 2022, Zhang 2021, Huang 2025) are small, heterogeneous and partly confounded by unmatched volume or intensity (e.g., Mann 2010). Huang 2025 rates its own certainty as low, and the expected advantage of REF5's closed loop over fixed loading could be close to zero.
- The deload evidence is a single RCT of complete cessation (Coleman 2024) plus consensus and survey data. Whether a reduced-load (not cessation) deload would help or hurt a low-volume, submaximal program like REF5 is untested.
- Bibliographic details of several sources were not fully re-verified: exact titles and author lists for Huang 2025, the 2023 IJSPP perceived velocity loss paper, Dello Stritto 2025, Bartolomei 2024 and Bonilla 2025, plus the full title of the ACSM 2026 position stand and the Bell 2023 Delphi paper. Findings were taken from the abstracts or full texts reviewed; titles marked 'descriptive' should be checked before formal citation.
- The 2x/wk volume concern for PULL, DL and OHP depends on how the athlete's actual frequency is distributed. At the observed ~1.75 hard SQ exposures per week, the real weekly sets may fall below the §19.3 table, but the session logs were not inspected in this dimension.
