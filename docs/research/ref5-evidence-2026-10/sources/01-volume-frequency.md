# 01. 볼륨·빈도 (`volume-frequency`)

> 2026-10-15 REF5 근거 평가 워크플로의 원자료다. 본문은 조사·검증 에이전트가 쓴 영어 원문 그대로다. 상태: confirmed(원문 확인), corrected(출처는 있으나 서지·수치를 고침, `검증 결과`를 쓸 것), unverifiable(확인 불가, 재확인 전 근거로 쓰지 말 것), misattributed(저자 귀속 오류).

## 조사 범위

How weekly training volume and frequency drive gains in maximal strength (hypertrophy as a secondary outcome), mainly in trained and intermediate lifters, applied to REF5 v1.4. This covers REF5's weekly hard sets per lift, its low per-session volume, the 2x/week case, and whether squatting 2–4x/week matters for strength once volume is held equal.

## 출처 (33건: confirmed 28, corrected 4, unverifiable 1)

### VF-01 · confirmed

**Pelland JC, Remmert JF, Robinson ZP, Hinson SR, Zourdos MC. The Resistance Training Dose Response: Meta-Regressions Exploring the Effects of Weekly Volume and Frequency on Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength Gains. Sports Med 2026;56(2):481-505 (online 4 Dec 2025). doi:10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w. SportRxiv preprint 460, Sept 2024**

- meta-analysis · 2025 · https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w
- 조사 요약: Multilevel Bayesian meta-regressions: 67 studies, 2,058 participants, 39 trained and 28 untrained samples. Counting indirect sets as 0.5 ('fractional') fit best. Strength vs volume: the best fit was reciprocal, with strong diminishing returns and a functional plateau. The marginal slope was +0.21% strength per weekly set (95% CrI 0.16–0.26). Strength 'efficiency tiers' were defined against a 3.96% smallest detectable effect: 1 fractional set/wk is the minimum effective dose; 2 is higher efficiency; 3–4 is intermediate (~2.25 extra sets needed per detectable gain); from ~5 sets, extra sets 'do not consistently enhance strength gains > SDES'. Strength vs frequency: positive with diminishing returns (slope +3.27% per session, CrI 2.74–3.84). The control-adjusted gain rose from 12.7% at 1 session/wk to 17.3% at 2, then returns diminished at an accelerating rate. Hypertrophy vs frequency was compatible with a negligible effect (91.3% probability slope >0). Hypertrophy vs volume: square-root shape; minimum effective dose 4 sets/wk; tiers 5–10, 11–18, 19–29 with no clear plateau. Median strength-study dose was only 6 sets/wk at 2 sessions/wk, ~78% of effects were failure-based, and mean duration was ~10 weeks. The authors warn that learning effects in novel 1RM tests may inflate the effect of very low doses. In well-trained powerlifters, 1–3 direct sets/wk were insufficient and 3–9 were sufficient.
- 검증 결과: Confirmed from the published abstract and Methods (67 studies, 2,058 participants, fractional counting best, SDES 3.96% for strength and 2.05% for hypertrophy). The detailed numbers were confirmed in the 2024 SportRxiv preprint (https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460) because the Springer page cut off before the Results; the final version may differ slightly. From the preprint: 39 trained and 28 untrained studies. The strength-volume fit was reciprocal ('strong diminishing returns and a functional plateau'), slope 0.21%/fractional set (CrI 0.16-0.26). Increments above the SDES were seen 'up to approximately 4 fractional weekly sets'. Strength tiers: MED 1 set; higher efficiency 2 sets (~0.75 extra set per detectable gain); intermediate 3-4 sets (~2.25 extra); from 5 sets extra sets 'do not consistently enhance strength gains > SDES'. Strength-frequency slope 3.27%/session (CrI 2.74-3.84), with control-adjusted gains of 12.72% at 1 session and 17.32% at 2; the authors say 'accelerating diminishing returns' occur after 2. Hypertrophy-frequency slope 0.32% (CrI -0.14 to 0.82), P(>0)=91.3%. Hypertrophy MED 4 sets; tiers 5-10, 11-18, 19-29, 30-42, 43+ unclear. Strength studies had a median of 6 sets/wk and 2 sessions/wk, 78.12% failure-based effects, and a mean duration of 10.42 weeks. Learning effects from novel 1RM tests were flagged. Two corrections to the stated finding. (1) The powerlifter result (1-3 direct sets/wk insufficient, 3-9 sufficient) is cited by Pelland from another paper (ref 132, apparently Androulakis-Korakakis 2021); it is not Pelland's own analysis. (2) The frequency models included a linear volume term, so the strength-frequency effect is volume-adjusted. The authors attribute it mainly to practice and test specificity.
- 검증 메모: PubMed/PMC were blocked by reCAPTCHA. Existence and metadata were confirmed via Europe PMC and Springer. The frequency effect being volume-adjusted matters for implication 3: Pelland does find an independent frequency effect on strength, largest from 1 to 2 sessions/wk.
- REF5 관련성: This is the most direct dose-response evidence for REF5's weekly set targets. SQ (6–11), BP (~6–10.5 fractional counting OHP as 0.5) and OHP (~4.5–7.5 fractional counting BP) all sit at or past the strength plateau zone. PULL at 2x/wk (~4 fractional) and DL (2–3) sit in the intermediate band. It also shows that frequency matters most going from 1 to 2 exposures per week.
- 조사 당시 표기: Pelland JC, Remmert JF, Robinson ZP, Hinson SR, Zourdos MC. 2025. The Resistance Training Dose Response: Meta-Regressions Exploring the Effects of Weekly Volume and Frequency on Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength Gains. Sports Medicine (online 4 Dec 2025; doi:10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w; SportRxiv preprint 2024)
- 조사 당시 URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/

### VF-02 · confirmed

**Remmert JF, Pelland JC, Robinson ZP, Hinson SR, Zourdos MC. 2025. Is There Too Much of a Good Thing? Meta-Regressions of the Effect of Per-Session Volume on Hypertrophy and Strength. SportRxiv preprint, doi:10.51224/SRXIV.537**

- meta-analysis · 2025 · https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537
- 조사 요약: Positive dose-response for per-session volume with diminishing returns. The point beyond which extra sets give no detectable superiority was ~2 direct sets per session for strength and ~11 fractional sets per session for hypertrophy. Diminishing returns are stronger for strength. Data at very high per-session volumes are scarce.
- 검증 결과: Preprint posted 2 Apr 2025, not peer reviewed. Positive dose-responses with diminishing returns. Point of undetectable outcome superiority: ~2 direct sets/session for strength and ~11 fractional sets/session for hypertrophy. Diminishing returns are stronger in the strength (direct) model. High per-session volume data are scarce (strength effects range 0-10 direct sets/session). Strength: 490 effects from 66 studies (n=2,020). Hypertrophy: 220 effects from 35 studies. The best-fit models adjusted only for duration and training status, not for weekly volume or frequency.
- 검증 메모: Because per-session volume was not adjusted for weekly volume or frequency, the relevance claim that extra weekly strength dose is 'better gained by adding exposures than by adding sets within a session' is an extrapolation, not a finding.
- REF5 관련성: Supports REF5 capping each lift at 2–3 work sets per session and keeping the whole session at ~10 sets. For strength, extra weekly dose is better gained by adding exposures than by adding sets within a session.
- 조사 당시 표기: Remmert JF, Pelland JC, Robinson ZP, Hinson SR, Zourdos MC. 2025. Is There Too Much of a Good Thing? Meta-Regressions of the Effect of Per-Session Volume on Hypertrophy and Strength. SportRxiv preprint (not peer reviewed), doi:10.51224/SRXIV.537

### VF-03 · confirmed

**Currier BS, D'Souza AC, Fiatarone Singh MA, Lowisz CV, Rawson ES, Schoenfeld BJ, et al., Phillips SM. ACSM Position Stand: Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance in Healthy Adults: An Overview of Reviews. Med Sci Sports Exerc 2026;58(4):851-872**

- position stand · 2026 · https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897
- 조사 요약: Overview of 137 systematic reviews (>30,000 participants). Strength was enhanced by loads ≥80% 1RM (dose-response), full range of motion, 2–3 sets per exercise per session, performing the lift at the start of the session, and ≥2 sessions/wk. Hypertrophy was enhanced by ≥10 sets/muscle/wk. Training to failure and periodization did not consistently affect outcomes. Citing a meta-regression, the full text says the dose-response 'plateaued and showed diminishing returns beyond ~2–3 sets/exercise for strength and ~18–20 weekly sets for hypertrophy', and advises at least 2 sets per exercise. The ACSM release notes that athletes and avid trainees need sport-specific plans.
- 검증 결과: 137 systematic reviews (>30,000 participants). Abstract: strength was enhanced by heavier loads (≥80% 1RM), a complete ROM, 2-3 sets, placing the lift at the beginning of the session, and ≥2 sessions/wk. Hypertrophy was enhanced by ≥10 sets/wk (Results: per muscle group) and by eccentric overload. Training to momentary fatigue and periodization did not consistently affect outcomes. The Discussion reports a dose-response 'that plateaued and showed diminishing returns beyond ~2-3 sets/exercise for strength ... and ~18-20 weekly sets for hypertrophy', and advises 'at least two sets per exercise'. The press release says athletes and avid trainees 'would ... require specific training plans'. The document also says 'much of the evidence synthesized here is from inexperienced trainees'.
- 검증 메모: Verified from the full-text PDF (fisiologiadelejercicio.com mirror), the ACSM page and the Newswise release. PubMed was blocked.
- REF5 관련성: REF5 meets every strength prescription element: SQ first, 3 sets per lift per session, ≥2 sessions/wk, H3/H2 at ~80–87%. It falls short of the ≥10 sets/wk hypertrophy guideline for several muscles at 2–3 sessions/wk. The finding on failure training supports REF5's choice to avoid intentional failure.
- 조사 당시 표기: Currier BS, D'Souza AC, Fiatarone Singh MA, Lowisz CV, Rawson ES, Schoenfeld BJ, et al., Phillips SM. 2026. American College of Sports Medicine Position Stand. Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance in Healthy Adults: An Overview of Reviews. Med Sci Sports Exerc 58(4):851–872
- 조사 당시 URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/

### VF-04 · confirmed

**Currier BS, Mcleod JC, Banfield L, et al., Phillips SM. 2023. Resistance training prescription for muscle strength and hypertrophy in healthy adults: a systematic review and Bayesian network meta-analysis. Br J Sports Med 57(18):1211-1220**

- meta-analysis · 2023 · https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/57/18/1211
- 조사 요약: Strength network of 178 RCTs (n=5,097). All prescriptions beat control. Higher-load (>80% 1RM) prescriptions maximised strength. The top-ranked strength prescription was higher-load, multi-set, 3x/week (SMD 1.60, 95% CrI 1.38–1.82), but many prescriptions had similar effects. The top-ranked hypertrophy prescription was higher-load, multi-set, 2x/week (SMD 0.66).
- 검증 결과: Strength network: 178 studies (n=5,097). Hypertrophy network: 119 studies (n=3,364). Higher-load (>80% 1RM) prescriptions maximised strength. The top strength prescription was higher-load, multiset, 3x/wk (SMD 1.60, CrI 1.38-1.82). The top hypertrophy prescription was higher-load, multiset, 2x/wk (SMD 0.66, CrI 0.47-0.85). 'The calculated effects of many prescriptions were similar.'
- REF5 관련성: REF5's model of heavy multi-set work 2–4 times a week is the category of prescription that ranks highest. Ranks are close, so 2x/week is not shown to be clearly inferior.
- 조사 당시 표기: Currier BS, Mcleod JC, Banfield L, Beyene J, Welton NJ, D'Souza AC, Keogh JAJ, Lin L, Coletta G, Yang A, Colenso-Semple L, Lau KJ, Verboom A, Phillips SM. 2023. Resistance training prescription for muscle strength and hypertrophy in healthy adults: a systematic review and Bayesian network meta-analysis. Br J Sports Med 57(18):1211–1220
- 조사 당시 URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37414459/

### VF-05 · confirmed

**Ralston GW, Kilgore L, Wyatt FB, Baker JS. 2017. The Effect of Weekly Set Volume on Strength Gain: A Meta-Analysis. Sports Med 47(12):2585-2601**

- meta-analysis · 2017 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28755103/
- 조사 요약: 9 studies, 61 treatment groups. High weekly sets beat low (≤5 sets per exercise per week): ES difference 0.18 (95% CI 0.06–0.30). For multi-joint lifts, high beat low by 0.18 (0.01–0.34). Medium sets marginally beat low (0.15). For well-trained lifters, medium or high weekly sets 'may be an appropriate dose'.
- 검증 결과: 9 studies, 61 treatment groups. High vs low weekly sets: ES 0.18 (0.06-0.30, p=0.003). Medium vs low: ES 0.15 (0.01-0.30, p=0.04). Multi-joint high vs low: ES 0.18 (0.01-0.34, p=0.04). Single-joint: ES 0.23. 'For well trained individuals, the use of either MWS or HWS may be an appropriate dose.' The stated finding omits one point: for 1RM outcomes specifically, high vs low was NOT significant (ES 0.14, -0.01 to 0.29, p=0.06). Low is ≤5 sets per exercise per week (as reported by Stronger By Science; the numeric cutoffs are not in the abstract).
- 검증 메모: Verified via the UWS research portal abstract. The non-significant 1RM-specific result weakens its use as evidence that >5 weekly sets beat ≤5 for 1RM strength.
- REF5 관련성: Older categorical evidence that more than 5 sets per lift per week beats ≤5. REF5's SQ and BP exceed 5 at 3–4x/wk. PULL, DL and OHP direct sets are ≤5 at every frequency.
- 조사 당시 표기: Ralston GW, Kilgore L, Wyatt FB, Baker JS. 2017. The Effect of Weekly Set Volume on Strength Gain: A Meta-Analysis. Sports Med 47(12):2585–2601

### VF-06 · corrected

**Ralston GW, Kilgore L, Wyatt FB, Buchan D, Baker JS. 2018. Weekly Training Frequency Effects on Strength Gain: A Meta-Analysis. Sports Med Open 4:36**

- meta-analysis · 2018 · https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-018-0149-9
- 조사 요약: 12 studies. Volume-equated, low frequency (1x/wk) vs high frequency (≥3x/wk) gave similar strength gains (ES 0.03, 95% CI −0.20 to 0.27). Upper body favoured high frequency (ES 0.48, 0.20–0.76). Lower body showed no significant difference (ES 0.21, p=0.22).
- 검증 결과: 12 studies (4 trained, 8 untrained). LF = 1 day/wk; HF = ≥3 days/wk. Volume-equated LF vs HF: ES 0.03 (-0.20 to 0.27, p=0.78), using 4 studies. Upper body HF > LF: ES 0.48 (0.20-0.76), 5 studies. Lower body: no significant difference, p=0.22. CORRECTIONS: (1) The upper- and lower-body subgroup analyses were NOT restricted to volume-equated studies. They used 5 studies each, adding Lera Orsatti et al. to the 4 volume-equated studies. (2) The lower-body ES is reported as +0.21 in the abstract but -0.21 in the Results, with CI -0.55 to 0.13. Only -0.21 fits that CI, so the abstract sign looks like a typo.
- 검증 메모: The relevance claim 'with volume held equal, lower-body did not benefit while upper-body pressing did' misdescribes the analysis. The upper/lower split mixes volume-equated and non-equated data.
- REF5 관련성: With volume held equal, lower-body (squat) strength did not benefit significantly from higher frequency, while upper-body pressing did.
- 조사 당시 URL: https://sportsmedicine-open.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40798-018-0149-9

### VF-07 · confirmed

**Grgic J, Schoenfeld BJ, Davies TB, Lazinica B, Krieger JW, Pedisic Z. 2018. Effect of Resistance Training Frequency on Gains in Muscular Strength: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Sports Med 48(5):1207-1220**

- meta-analysis · 2018 · https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-018-0872-x
- 조사 요약: 22 studies. ES rose with frequency: 0.74, 0.82, 0.93 and 1.08 for 1, 2, 3 and 4+ sessions/wk (p=0.003). In volume-equated studies frequency had no significant effect (p=0.421; ES 0.53, 0.80, 0.64 for 1/2/3x). Multi-joint lifts p<0.001; upper body p=0.004; lower body not significant (p=0.070). The authors concluded the benefit is mostly carried by extra volume. Most included studies used untrained participants.
- 검증 결과: 22 studies. ES 0.74, 0.82, 0.93 and 1.08 for 1, 2, 3 and 4+ sessions/wk (p=0.003). Volume-equated: no significant frequency effect (p=0.421). Multi-joint p<0.001; single-joint p=0.324; upper body p=0.004; lower body p=0.070. 'These effects seem to be primarily driven by training volume.' 'Most of the current studies were performed in untrained participants.'
- 검증 메모: The per-frequency ES values within volume-equated studies (0.53, 0.80, 0.64) are not in the abstract and could not be verified. The upper/lower and multi-joint subgroup results come from all studies, not only volume-equated ones.
- REF5 관련성: Frequency mainly acts as a way to add volume. In volume-equated designs the squat-specific benefit of extra frequency is not established.
- 조사 당시 표기: Grgic J, Schoenfeld BJ, Davies TB, Lazinica B, Krieger JW, Pedisic Z. 2018. Effect of Resistance Training Frequency on Gains in Muscular Strength: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Sports Med 48(5):1207–1220
- 조사 당시 URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29470825/

### VF-08 · confirmed

**Cuthbert M, Haff GG, Arent SM, Ripley N, McMahon JJ, Evans M, Comfort P. 2021. Effects of Variations in Resistance Training Frequency on Strength Development in Well-Trained Populations... Sports Med 51(9):1967-1982**

- meta-analysis · 2021 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33886099/
- 조사 요약: In well-trained participants doing compound lifts over 6–12 weeks, maximal strength showed no clear differences between frequencies. Only small between-frequency effects were seen (upper g ≤0.58, lower g ≤0.45). The authors suggest volume can be spread across shorter, more frequent sessions ('micro-dosing').
- 검증 결과: Well-trained participants, compound exercises, at least two frequency groups. Small between-frequency effects: upper body g ≤0.58, lower body g ≤0.45. No clear differences in maximal strength between frequencies over 6-12 weeks. The authors suggest spreading volume over shorter, more frequent sessions, 'allowing for a micro-dosing of the strength stimuli'.
- 검증 메모: Verified via the ECU repository abstract.
- REF5 관련성: This is the trained-population evidence. Distributing volume across REF5's irregular 2–4 sessions should not cost strength, but higher frequency on its own is not shown to add any.
- 조사 당시 표기: Cuthbert M, Haff GG, Arent SM, Ripley N, McMahon JJ, Evans M, Comfort P. 2021. ### VF-09 · corrected

**Nuckols G. 2018. Training Frequency for Strength Development: What the Data Say. Stronger By Science**

- coaching source · 2018 · https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=46685
- 조사 요약: Coach-author meta-analysis, not peer reviewed: 13 volume-matched studies, 328 subjects. Higher frequency gave ~20–23% faster gains overall, with a small effect (d≈0.27). In trained subjects d=0.42, p=0.046. Upper-body pressing gained +0.51%/wk per extra weekly day (≈+28% rate; significant). Quad-dominant lower-body lifts gained only +0.10%/wk per extra day (≈+5%; p=0.33, not significant). Nuckols also argues that real-world frequency raises tolerable volume (e.g. 4–5 squat sets ×3 is easier than 10–15 in one session).
- 검증 결과: Published 30 Jul 2018 (modified 2020). This is coaching analysis, not peer reviewed. Original analysis: 13 volume-matched studies, 328 subjects, 31 comparisons. Higher frequency gave ~23% more total gain (~21% faster weekly rate), d=0.265 (CI 0.08-0.45). Trained subjects: d=0.42 (0.03-0.81), p=0.046. Upper-body pressing: +0.51%/wk per extra weekly day (~28% faster, p=0.019). Quad-dominant lower body: +0.10%/wk per extra day (~5%, p=0.33). CORRECTION: the article's own correction notice drops Barcelos (not volume-matched), leaving 12 studies and revising the overall effect to d=0.211. Example given: 4 hard squat sets x3/wk is 'very doable' vs 10 hard sets in one workout.
- 검증 메모: The overall effect should be cited as the corrected d≈0.21, not ≈0.27.
- REF5 관련성: Supports frequent bench exposure (BP appears in every REF5 session). For the squat, adding exposures beyond ~2 matters little once volume is matched. Value for the squat comes mainly from spreading volume across sessions. This is coaching analysis, not peer reviewed.
- 조사 당시 표기: Nuckols G. 2018. Training Frequency for Strength Development: What the Data Say. Stronger By Science (author's site)

### VF-10 · confirmed

**Nuckols G. 2021. Research Spotlight: How does training frequency affect strength gains? Stronger By Science**

- coaching source · 2021 · https://www.strongerbyscience.com/research-spotlight-frequency/
- 조사 요약: After Cuthbert 2021, Nuckols revised his view: the weight of evidence trended toward 'no real difference' in trained lifters. Higher frequency remains practically useful for distributing volume and keeping set quality high. With fewer than ~10 sets per muscle per week, 1x/week is 'probably perfectly fine'. Slow recoverers may do better on lower frequency than on very low per-session volume.
- 검증 결과: Published 14 Jul 2021; spotlights Cuthbert 2021. Since 2018 'the weight of the evidence has trended toward no real difference'. Higher frequencies may allow higher volumes and/or higher average training quality. With fewer than ~10 sets per muscle group per week, once weekly 'is probably perfectly fine'. Slower recoverers tend to do better on lower frequencies than on forcing per-session volume very low. This is coaching opinion.
- REF5 관련성: This coaching opinion matches REF5's use of frequency as a vehicle for volume rather than as a goal in itself. It also suggests the hard-squat spacing cap costs little.
- 조사 당시 표기: Nuckols G. 2021. Research Spotlight: How does training frequency affect strength gains? Stronger By Science (author's site)

### VF-11 · confirmed

### VF-12 · confirmed

### VF-13 · confirmed

**Johnsen E, van den Tillaar R. 2021. Effects of training frequency on muscular strength for trained men under volume matched conditions. PeerJ 9:e10781**

- RCT · 2021 · https://peerj.com/articles/10781/
- 조사 요약: 21 experienced men, 8 weeks, each muscle trained 2 vs 4 sessions/wk with volume equated. Squat +13.25 vs +14.31 kg (p=0.640); bench +7.75 vs +8.86 kg (p=0.431). RPE after squats evolved differently between groups (interaction p=0.002). The authors suggest spreading volume to limit perceived exertion.
- 검증 결과: 21 experienced trained men, 8 weeks. Each muscle group trained 2x/wk (SPLIT, n=10) vs 4x/wk (FULLBODY, n=11), volume equated. Squat +13.25 vs +14.31 kg (p=0.640); bench +7.75 vs +8.86 kg (p=0.431). Only RPE after squats showed a group x time interaction (p=0.002). The authors suggest spreading volume may limit RPE increases, especially after squats.
- REF5 관련성: The squat comparison of 2 vs 4 sessions maps closely onto REF5's 2–4x/week range: no strength difference. Perceived-effort data mildly favour spreading volume across more sessions, which fits REF5's RPE 7–8 target.

Minimal-dose RT 'may also limit negative affective responses' associated with higher volumes that may hurt adherence. Evidence comes largely from general and older populations.
- REF5 관련성: Supports REF5's low-volume, adherence-oriented design for a lifter with poor recovery and an irregular schedule (constraints C1, C2).
- 조사 당시 표기: Fyfe JJ, Hamilton DL, Daly RM. 2022. Minimal-Dose Resistance Training for Improving Muscle Mass, Strength, and Function: A Narrative Review of Current Evidence and Practical Considerations. Sports Med 52:463–479
- 조사 당시 URL: https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-021-01605-8

### VF-31 · confirmed

### VF-33 · confirmed

**ADDITIONAL (surfaced during checking, not in original findings): Tao M, Yin M, Zhong Y, et al., Hackett D, Li Y. 2026. - 검증 단계에서 추가된 출처
- 검증 결과: 47 studies, 1,665 participants, three-level meta-analysis. Higher vs lower frequency gave a small but significant strength advantage (|g|=0.17, I²=0%, p<0.0001). ## REF5 함의: 검증 후 유지

- Per-session structure (2-3 sets per main lift, ~10 work sets, SQ first) is supported. ACSM 2026 abstract: 2-3 sets, lift at the start of the session, ≥2 sessions/wk. Remmert 2025 preprint: strength PUOS ~2 direct sets/session. Schoenfeld 2019: 1 = 3 = 5 sets for squat/bench 1RM in trained men. Caveat: Remmert did not adjust for weekly volume, and Pedersen 2024's 'fresh squat' mechanism is interpretation, not a reported finding.
- SQ weekly volume (6/9/~11 direct sets) is adequate but not a proven maximum. Pelland 2025 (preprint numbers) shows increments above the SDES only up to ~4 fractional sets. Enes 2024 IJSM: 6 = 9 > 3. Enes 2024 MSSE: more lower-limb volume gave more squat 1RM in trained men at 2x/wk, but this is confounded with total quad volume at far higher absolute volumes. Weekly squat volume as the first lever in a §26-style proposal if SQ stalls remains reasonable.
- Squatting more than ~2x/week adds little once volume is matched. Directly supported by the confirmed volume-equated RCTs in trained lifters: Colquhoun 2018 (3 vs 6), Saric 2019 (3 vs 6), Johnsen & van den Tillaar 2021 (2 vs 4), Hamarsland 2022 (2 vs 4). Also supported by Cuthbert 2021 and Pelland 2025 (largest frequency step is 1 to 2 sessions, then accelerating diminishing returns). Pelland attributes the frequency effect to practice and test specificity, which lends some support to the 'technical practice' hypothesis.
- The ~1.75 hard squat starts per week plus V squats are unlikely to cost meaningful strength. Total squat exposures are ≥2/wk, where Pelland's curve has already captured most of the frequency benefit.
- BP in every session is supported as harmless, and it adds volume and practice. Frequency never hurt strength in any confirmed study, and Johnsen 2021 suggests spreading volume lowers perceived exertion.
- DL at 2-3 sets/wk is consistent with low priority. Pelland bands, Spiering 2021 (maintenance with intensity kept; data are for younger general adults, not athletes), Räntilä 2026 (extra volume did not help and the deadlift favoured lower volume) and Starting Strength's minimal deadlift volume all fit. Progress is predicted to be slow.
- OHP is supported as low priority under Pelland's fractional counting. The binding constraints are the cap and the 2.5 kg step, not volume.
- The 2x/week case is supported for SQ and BP. ACSM ≥2 sessions/wk; Currier 2023 (heavy multi-set at 2-3x/wk ranks top, many prescriptions similar); Rhea 2003 (trained optimum at 2 days/wk at 80% 1RM).
- The concern that the micro limit understimulates at 4x/week is largely contradicted for strength. Volume-equated frequency above 3 adds nothing measurable (Colquhoun, Saric), and SQ/BP weekly sets at 4x are past Pelland's strength plateau.
- The prediction of low hypertrophy under the constraints is supported. ACSM: ≥10 sets/muscle/wk for hypertrophy. Schoenfeld 2017: +0.37% per weekly set. Robinson 2024: hypertrophy rises closer to failure while strength does not. Murphy & Koehler 2022: deficits impair lean mass but not strength. The cost comes from the constraints, not the structure.
- Volume audit before lowering the standard in the stagnation path is supported. Realized weekly sets predict strength gain (Pelland 2025; both Enes 2024 papers). Missed sessions lowering realized sets is a plausible underload cause to check first. This involves no change to FAIL 0 / HOLD ≤1 / volume veto.
- No change to volume or frequency is indicated now, given every logged window ended INCREASE. This is consistent with the meta-regression dose estimates, though it is observation, not proof.

## REF5 함의: 검증에서 약화

- 'In trained lifters with volume held equal, squat frequency shows no reliable independent effect' is overstated as worded. (a) Ralston 2018's lower-body (and upper-body) subgroup was NOT volume-equated (5 studies including one non-equated), and its lower-body ES sign is inconsistent (abstract +0.21, Results -0.21). Drop it as volume-equated evidence. (b) Grgic 2018's lower-body p=0.07 comes from all studies, not volume-equated ones. (c) The two most recent meta-analyses, Pelland 2025 (frequency model adjusted for volume) and Tao 2026 (47 studies, |g|=0.17, 'frequency independently predicts strength gains'), both find a small independent frequency effect on strength, concentrated going from 1 to 2 sessions. The practical conclusion for REF5 (≥2 squat exposures/wk is enough) survives. The blanket 'no independent effect' statement does not.
- 'Upper-body pressing is where frequency evidence is most positive' relies partly on subgroups that are not volume-equated (Grgic 2018 upper-body p=0.004 across all studies; Ralston 2018 ES 0.48 including a non-equated study). The volume-matched support is Nuckols 2018 (coaching analysis; its overall effect was corrected down from d=0.265 to d=0.211) and Cuthbert 2021 (small effects, upper g ≤0.58, no clear difference). The BP-every-session rule is still fine, but the evidence for a pressing-specific frequency benefit is weaker than stated.
- Swinton 2024 was miscited. There is no ~70% 'inflection'; the strength-load curve has a 'reduced incline between 65 and 100%'. The 'few key exercises over many at higher frequency' recommendation was not found. The claim that the 70/82.5 BP volume load and V squats are 'moderate' strength stimuli 'near the inflection' has no support from this paper. Swinton supports heavy loads and multiple sets per session, and finds frequency non-predictive.
- PULL under-dosing at 2x/week (3 direct sets) is weaker than stated. It rests on Enes 2024 IJSM (3 < 6 sets for squat and bench), Ralston 2017 and Peterson 2004. But Schoenfeld 2019 found 3 weekly squat/bench sets (1 set x 3 sessions, to failure) produced 1RM gains equal to 9 and 15 sets in trained men. Androulakis-Korakakis 2020 shows single sets still give significant trained gains. Ralston 2017's 1RM-specific high-vs-low comparison was non-significant (p=0.06). Peterson 2004's '8 sets per muscle group' is not confirmed to be per week (possibly per session). So '3 weekly sets is suboptimal' is contested even for squat and bench, and the extrapolation to weighted pull-ups is weaker still. The testable expectation (PULL windows run longest, first HOLD/FAIL in PULL) remains a reasonable hypothesis, not an evidence-based prediction.
- The ~5-6 heavy squat sets/wk being 'the minimum effective heavy dose for powerlifters' (Androulakis-Korakakis 2021) rests mainly on expert interviews and surveys plus two small quasi-randomized exploratory 6-week interventions (n=25). Treat it as expert consensus, not trial evidence. The reported back-off sets were at ~80% of the day's single, not of 1RM. Pelland's 'powerlifters: 1-3 sets insufficient, 3-9 sufficient' is a citation of this same paper, not independent corroboration.
- 'Pedersen 2024 suggests a squat done fresh in a short session beats one done after a long session': the exercise order within the LOW session could not be verified, and the paper reports a frequency (session-distribution) effect, not an order or fatigue mechanism. Keep this as interpretation. Loads were also 6-12RM, not heavy low-rep work.
- 'For strength, extra weekly dose is better gained by adding exposures than by adding sets within a session' (from Remmert 2025): this is extrapolation. The per-session models were not adjusted for weekly volume or frequency, and the paper is an unreviewed preprint.
- Enes 2024 MSSE as evidence that weekly SQUAT volume specifically is the lever: the added sets were total lower-limb volume at very high absolute doses (2x/wk). Squat-specific set counts (~8/14/18) are not in the abstract and could not be verified. It shows lower-body volume can still drive squat 1RM in trained men. It does not isolate squat sets.
- Pelland 2025's detailed numbers (tiers, slopes, 12.7%→17.3%, 78% failure, median 6 sets) were verified against the 2024 SportRxiv preprint because the published full text could not be fully read. The published version may differ slightly, so cite those numbers as 'preprint values consistent with the published abstract'.
- Verification gaps (tool limits, not evidence problems): PubMed/PMC were blocked by reCAPTCHA; Europe PMC, Crossref search and doi.org hit rate limits on some requests; and the shared web-search budget ran out mid-check. Peterson 2004's abstract, Grgic 2018's volume-equated per-frequency ES values (0.53/0.80/0.64), Murphy & Koehler's exact ES values, and Saric 2019's supplement pages could not be directly confirmed.

## REF5 함의: 조사 단계 원안

- Per-session structure (3 sets per main lift, ~10 work sets per session, SQ first): SUPPORTED by direct evidence. The 2026 ACSM position stand recommends 2–3 sets per exercise per session for strength, with the key lift at the start of the session. Remmert 2025 puts the strength plateau at ~2 direct sets per session. Schoenfeld 2019 (trained men) found 1 vs 3 vs 5 sets per exercise gave equal squat and bench strength gains. Pedersen 2024 suggests a squat done fresh in a short session beats one done after a long session. No change indicated.
- SQ weekly volume (spec §19.3: 6 / 9 / ~11 direct sets at 2 / 3 / 4 sessions per week): SUPPORTED as adequate, but NOT the maximum. Pelland 2025 puts strength gains at a functional plateau from about 5 fractional sets per week (extra sets no longer reliably exceed 3.96%). Enes 2024 (IJSM, trained athletes) found 6 = 9 > 3 weekly squat sets, and REF5 is at or above 6 at every frequency. However, Enes 2024 (MSSE, lifters squatting 1.7× bodyweight) found more squat volume still produced more squat strength. So 'more sets cannot help' is not established for trained lower bodies. If SQ stalls, weekly squat volume is the best-supported single lever. It belongs in a §26-style proposal, one change at a time, weighed against C2 recovery and the knee. It is not a current directive, because every logged window ended INCREASE.
- SQ hard-exposure rule (≥48 h since the last hard start and fewer than 2 hard starts per 168 h; otherwise V): SUPPORTED. In trained lifters with volume held equal, squat frequency shows no reliable independent effect. Evidence: Colquhoun 2018 (3 vs 6 sessions: +16.8 vs +16.7 kg), Saric 2019 (3 vs 6), Johnsen & van den Tillaar 2021 (2 vs 4: +13.25 vs +14.31 kg, p=0.64), Hamarsland 2022 (2 vs 4), Cuthbert 2021, Grgic 2018 lower body (p=0.07) and Ralston 2018 lower body (n.s.). Pelland 2025 finds a frequency effect for strength, but its largest step is from 1 to 2 exposures per week (12.7% to 17.3%), with diminishing returns after. The realized ~1.75 hard exposures per week is therefore unlikely to cost meaningful squat strength. The resulting ~5–6 heavy sets per week sits at the minimum effective heavy dose reported for powerlifters (Androulakis-Korakakis 2021: 3–6 sets of 1–5 reps above 80%). That is enough, but with little margin, which is extrapolation for a non-powerlifter.
- The question of whether squatting 2–4x/week matters independent of volume: the evidence says LITTLE beyond 2x/week. That conclusion is direct for volume-equated designs and extrapolated for REF5's case. In REF5, frequency is mainly a way to spread volume and keep each set good quality, consistent with Nuckols 2018 and 2021 (coaching analysis). A plausible but unproven extra benefit is technical practice of the high-bar pattern. The evidence is SILENT on irregular spacing (clusters and gaps): every frequency study used regular schedules.
- BP in every normal and micro session (focus 3×3 or volume 2×5; 5–9 direct sets, ~6–10.5 counting OHP as half sets): SUPPORTED. Upper-body pressing is where frequency evidence is most positive. Grgic 2018 found a significant upper-body effect (p=0.004). Ralston 2018 found high vs low frequency gave ES 0.48 for the upper body. Nuckols 2018 estimated +28% faster pressing gains per extra weekly day; that is coaching analysis. The 70/82.5 derived volume load sits around the 70% inflection (Swinton 2024), so it is a moderate stimulus that mainly adds volume and practice.
- PULL in v1.4 (3 direct sets/week at 2x, ~4–6 at 3–4x, plus OAP pair-sets counted here as half-sets, giving ~4–7.5 fractional): this is the stream where the evidence most suggests UNDER-DOSING, but only by extrapolation. No trial studies weighted pull-ups. At 2x/week, PULL gets one heavy exposure and 3 direct sets per week. That equals the 3-set condition that underperformed in trained squat and bench (Enes 2024 IJSM). It is below Ralston 2017's >5 sets per week and Peterson 2004's ~8 sets per muscle. It also forfeits the upper-body frequency benefit seen for pressing. This matches spec §22.4 item 9. Testable expectation: PULL windows will run longest, and a first HOLD/FAIL is most likely to appear in PULL. If PULL stagnates, the existing revert input (v1.3 2×6) is the single protocol change consistent with this evidence, not loosening the judgment criteria. OAP's own progression is outside this dimension.
- DL 2–3 sets per week at ~72.5–75 (≈73–75% of REF): CONSISTENT with the program's priorities (C7 cap), but the evidence predicts slow DL progress. In Pelland 2025, 2–3 sets falls in the 'higher/intermediate efficiency' band. Loads are below ACSM's ≥80% strength threshold, and Pelland's scheme gives no indirect credit from squats toward the deadlift. Spiering 2021 indicates the deadlift will at least be maintained, and Räntilä 2026 shows more deadlift volume can blunt deadlift gains. Low deadlift volume also matches classic coaching practice (Starting Strength 1×5). This rule is SUPPORTED as a secondary stream.
- OHP 2–3 sets of 2×6 at 32.5 (~65% of the control REF of 50): SUPPORTED as low priority. Bench sets give it roughly 4.5–7.5 fractional sets, which Pelland's fractional model treats as at or past the plateau. The binding constraints are the cap and the 2.5 kg step (§22.4 item 5), not volume. The evidence is SILENT on whether the cap freezes progress.
- The 2x/week case: SUPPORTED for SQ and BP. Six squat and 5 bench direct sets meet or nearly meet the Pelland plateau and the Enes 6-set threshold, and ACSM's ≥2 sessions per week is met. Currier 2023 ranked heavy multi-set training at 2x/week near the top, and Rhea 2003 and Peterson 2004 put the trained optimum at 2 days per week. WEAK for PULL (3 direct sets, about one heavy exposure per week), and DL and OHP are minimal at 2 sets each. A process cost the evidence does not address: because focus lifts alternate, a 4-exposure BP or PULL window takes about 4 calendar weeks at 2x/week.
- Concern that the micro limit understimulates at 4x/week (§22.4 item 2): LARGELY CONTRADICTED for strength. At 4x/week, SQ is already at ~11 direct sets and BP at 8–9, beyond Pelland's strength plateau. Lower-body frequency above 3 adds little at equal volume (Colquhoun, Saric). Any understimulation would show up in hypertrophy, not strength.
- Hypertrophy as a secondary aim (no accessories, sets ending at the first slowdown, slow cut): the evidence predicts LOW muscle gain. The cost comes from the constraints, not from REF5's structure, and is not a recommendation. Quads get ~7–12.5 fractional sets per week. Pecs get ~5–9. Lats get ~3–6 direct plus OAP. This is at or below ACSM's ≥10 sets per muscle per week. The sets also end further from failure than in the studies that built the volume curves (Robinson 2024). Murphy & Koehler 2022 show energy deficits blunt lean-mass gain but not strength. Extrapolation, not evidence: G1 needs roughly a 70% rise in squat 1RM (≈103 to 175 kg) at a lower bodyweight. Beyond the intermediate stage this probably requires meaningful quad and glute growth. Over a multi-year horizon, the low hypertrophy dose may become rate-limiting. If so, the stagnation procedure's volume audit is where it will show up.
- Stagnation path (two consecutive 'maintain' windows → stagnation micro → reassessment → lower the direct standard) and structureReview: the evidence SUPPORTS keeping the volume audit (realized weekly sets vs §19.3) before any decrease. Enes 2024 (both papers) and Pelland 2025 show realized weekly sets and exposures predict strength gain. Missed sessions or frequent micros can push SQ or BP below ~6 direct sets per week and PULL to ~3, which is a plausible cause of underload. This should be checked before lowering the standard is read as overload. This is a consideration for interpreting stagnation, not a change to the FAIL 0 / HOLD ≤1 / volume-veto criteria.
- Real data check, read as observation rather than proof. 28 sessions; v1.4 averaged ~2.9 sessions/week with ~1.75 hard squats/week. Every main-lift window ended INCREASE with no HOLD or FAIL. For a lifter squatting ~1.37× bodyweight, this matches the meta-regression prediction that the current realized dose is enough for continued progress at present loads. The evidence therefore gives no reason to change volume or frequency now. The first evidence-based trigger for a change would be repeated HOLD/FAIL or two maintain windows, most likely in PULL first.

## 불확실성

- Population mismatch: the dose-response meta-regressions (Pelland 2025: median 6 sets/week, ~10 weeks, ~78% failure-based protocols, many untrained samples doing novel 1RM tests) may understate what an intermediate lifter on a one-year plateau needs. Pelland itself notes that in well-trained powerlifters 1–3 weekly sets were insufficient. Enes 2024 (MSSE) shows trained lower bodies still respond to more volume.
- Pelland's 'plateau' means extra sets no longer exceed a 3.96% smallest detectable effect over ~10 weeks. Over the multi-year horizon of G1, smaller cumulative gains from more volume could still matter. No study runs longer than about 12–26 weeks.
- How to count REF5's sets is unvalidated. Sets end at the first obvious slowdown (an unknown RIR, perhaps 1–4). V squats and BP volume are at ~70% 1RM. OAP is unilateral and self-assisted, and its weighting toward weighted-pull-up strength is assumed here to be 0.5 per pair-set. Overlaps (DL→SQ, BP↔OHP, climbing→PULL) use Pelland's 0.5 fractional rule, which was fitted for other exercise pairs. Pelland's scheme does not count deadlifts toward squat or squats toward deadlift.
- No trial examines weighted pull-up or one-arm pull-up volume or frequency. All PULL conclusions are extrapolated from bench, squat and pulldown data.
- Frequency studies use regular schedules and mostly volume-equated designs. REF5's irregular clusters and gaps (e.g. the recent 8-day gap), its density-driven micro sessions, and the fact that frequency also changes volume in REF5 are not directly studied.
- Pedersen 2024's frequency benefit reflects muscle-group frequency and per-session fatigue, with each exercise done once per week. It should not be read as evidence that squatting more often helps.
- Individual variation in recovery is large. Nuckols's view that slow recoverers do better on lower frequency is coaching opinion. The user's poor sleep and nutrition (C2), climbing, and the undiagnosed quadriceps-tendon pain may cap tolerable squat volume and frequency below the averages, and no evidence in this dimension quantifies that cap.
- Energy-deficit effects (Murphy & Koehler 2022) come from mostly non-athlete studies with larger deficits than the user's planned ~0.25% bodyweight per week. How much they blunt hypertrophy at this mild deficit is uncertain.
- Logged bodyweight appears to be a default (75 kg every session). PULL total-load dose (bodyweight + added) may therefore be mis-measured, which affects any volume or intensity reading for PULL.
- The 2026 ACSM figures ('~2–3 sets/exercise' plateau, '≥10 sets/wk' for hypertrophy) are aimed at healthy adults in general. The ACSM release itself says avid trainees and athletes need specific plans.
- The year for the Starting Strength program page is approximate (undated web page). The Remmert 2025 per-session analysis is a preprint and not yet peer reviewed.
