# 08. 목표 현실성 (`goal-feasibility`)

> 2026-10-15 REF5 근거 평가 워크플로의 원자료다. 본문은 조사·검증 에이전트가 쓴 영어 원문 그대로다. 상태: confirmed(원문 확인), corrected(출처는 있으나 서지·수치를 고침, `검증 결과`를 쓸 것), unverifiable(확인 불가, 재확인 전 근거로 쓰지 말 것), misattributed(저자 귀속 오류).

## 조사 범위

Goal feasibility and strength standards: how rare and how far away a 175 kg high-bar squat at about 70 kg (G1) and a one-arm chin-up (G6) are, and whether REF5's maximum progression rate is the binding constraint

## 출처 (32건: confirmed 19, corrected 8, unverifiable 5)

### GF-01 · corrected

**Latella C, van den Hoek D, Wolf M, Androulakis-Korakakis P, Fisher JP, Steele J. Using powerlifting athletes to determine strength adaptations across ages in males and females: a longitudinal growth modelling approach. Sports Med 2024;54(3):753-774 (online 7 Dec 2023)**

- cohort · 2023 · https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-023-01962-6
- 조사 요약: Retrospective OpenPowerlifting (IPF, raw) data: 9,259 lifters, 46,066 observations, up to about 17 years. Strength rose about 7.5-12.5% above the first-meet baseline in year 1 and only about 12.5-20% after 10 years, so gains are front-loaded and then plateau. The squat progressed fastest of the three lifts. Lighter and younger lifters progressed faster in percentage terms. The authors judged survivorship bias unlikely to explain the plateau.
- 검증 결과: The abstract confirms that gains are front-loaded: about 7.5-12.5% in the first year and up to about 20% after 10 years. Females tended to progress faster, possibly because of lower baseline strength. Female Masters 3-4 lifters (over 59) still improved about 2.5-5%. Male Masters 4 lifters (over 69) lost about 0.35% per year. Body mass was a time-varying covariate. The accessible page does NOT state the sample size (9,259 lifters / 46,066 observations / 17 years), which lift progressed fastest, that lighter lifters progressed faster, drug-tested or IPF status, or any judgment on survivorship bias. These parts could not be verified.
- 검증 메모: Read on the Springer page (abstract only, paywalled). Authors, journal, volume and pages match. Write 'up to ~20% after 10 years', not '12.5-20%'. Do not cite the survivorship-bias claim or the 'tested' label from this paper unless the full text is checked.
- REF5 관련성: The best large-scale direct evidence on long-term progression in trained lifters. A +70% squat gain (103 to 175 kg) is far outside the 10-year trajectory typical of competitive raw lifters once they start competing.
- 조사 당시 표기: Latella C, van den Hoek D, Wolf M, Androulakis-Korakakis P, Fisher JP, Steele J. Using powerlifting athletes to determine strength adaptations across ages in males and females: a longitudinal growth modelling approach. Sports Medicine 2024;54(3):753-774 (online 2023)

### GF-02 · corrected

**Nuckols G. How Sex, Strength, and Age Affect Strength Gains in Powerlifters. Stronger by Science, 5 July 2018 (modified 26 Jan 2020)**

- coaching source · 2018 · https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=46506
- 조사 요약: Analysis of about 19,749 OpenPowerlifting lifters and 70,117 meet-to-meet intervals. Men's median gain was 0.576% per month (about 7% per year). Among lifters with 5 or more meets, the median rate fell from 0.78% per month (meet 1 to 2) to 0.26% per month (meet 4 to 5), roughly 9% per year down to 3% per year, over about 770 days. Initial strength, age and sex together explained only about 11% of the variance. The author says competition data probably overstate true progress.
- 검증 결과: The analysis used 19,749 lifters and 70,117 meet-to-meet results from OpenPowerlifting. Men averaged a MEAN (not median) gain of 0.576% per month (SD 1.475%; about 7% per year). For the 4,312 lifters with 5 or more meets, the MEDIAN rate fell from 0.78% per month (meet 1 to 2) to 0.26% per month (meet 4 to 5), with meet 5 about 770 days after meet 1. Initial strength, age and sex together explained 11.4% of the variance. The author states the rates 'probably overstate the true rates of progress'.
- 검증 메모: The only correction is mean versus median for 0.576% per month. Everything else matches. This is a coaching analysis of competition data, not peer-reviewed.
- REF5 관련성: Gives a realistic adaptation-rate band (about 3-7% per year) to set against REF5's maximum progression rate.
- 조사 당시 표기: Nuckols G. How Sex, Strength, and Age Affect Strength Gains in Powerlifters. Stronger by Science, 5 July 2018 (updated 2020)

### GF-03 · confirmed

**Nuckols G. Modeling strength gains over time. Stronger by Science, 26 Oct 2022**

- coaching source · 2022 · https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=57447
- 조사 요약: Combines data from about 15,000 gym-chain trainees (up to about 7 years), about 10,000 OpenPowerlifting lifters and an r/weightroom survey. Strength follows a linear-log pattern: each equally spaced milestone takes about twice as long as the one before (for example, +10 kg in 1 month, then 2, 4 and 8 months).
- 검증 결과: Three datasets were combined: about 15,000 clients of a Dutch gym chain (once weekly, one set to failure on each of six machine exercises, up to about 7 years; this is the Steele et al. RQES dataset, cited as ref 1), 10,000 random OpenPowerlifting lifters, and about 1,000 r/weightroom survey respondents. All three follow a linear-log pattern. Each equally spaced milestone probably takes about twice as long as the previous one (10 kg in 1 month, then about 2, 4 and 8 months). This is framed as a rough prediction.
- 검증 메모: The article does NOT describe a hard plateau. It says progress keeps slowing logarithmically and that 'there are always strength gains to be made' until injury or ageing intervenes.
- REF5 관련성: Coaching-level model implying that constant-step progression (+2.5 kg per window) will increasingly outrun real adaptation as the lifter advances.

### GF-04 · confirmed

**Latella C, Owen PJ, Davies T, Spathis J, Mallard A, van den Hoek D. Long-term adaptations in the squat, bench press, and deadlift: assessing strength gain in powerlifting athletes. Med Sci Sports Exerc 2022;54(5):841-850**

- cohort · 2022 · https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000002858
- 조사 요약: Data from 1,896 competitors across 407 meets, 2003-2018. Maximum strength gain was 20.2-25.4 kg per year for the squat, 18.1-21.1 for the deadlift and 10.5-12.8 for the bench. Gains did not differ by sex. The strongest male quartile gained more slowly.
- 검증 결과: 407 meets, 1,896 unique competitors (about 625 female and 1,270 male), 2003-2018. Maximum strength adaptations were squat 20.2-25.4 kg per year and deadlift 18.1-21.1 kg per year, versus bench 10.5-12.8 kg per year (P<=0.001). Absolute and relative changes did not differ between sexes. Males in the strongest quartile generally gained more slowly per day. Female gains did not differ by quartile.
- 검증 메모: The publisher DOI page failed. The abstract was verified through Crossref metadata (api.crossref.org) and the numbers match exactly.
- REF5 관련성: Upper-bound reference: even competitive powerlifters' maximal yearly squat gains (about 20-25 kg per year, mostly early in their careers) are well below REF5's theoretical ceiling of about 40-55 kg per year in REF terms.

### GF-05 · confirmed

**Latella C, Teo W-P, Spathis J, van den Hoek D. Long-term strength adaptation: a 15-year analysis of powerlifting athletes. J Strength Cond Res 2020;34(9):2412-2418**

- cohort · 2020 · https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000003657
- 조사 요약: 1,271 male lifters averaged 0.15 kg per day of total (about 55 kg per year across squat, bench and deadlift). The strongest males gained 0.102 kg per day versus 0.211 for the weakest, suggesting a ceiling effect.
- 검증 결과: 1,897 lifters (626 female, 1,271 male), 2003-2018. Males gained 0.15 +/- 0.44 kg per day of total (about 55 kg per year of total). Females gained 0.12 +/- 0.69 kg per day (no significant sex difference). The strongest male quartile gained 0.102 kg per day versus 0.211 for the weakest (p=0.010), interpreted as a possible ceiling effect. Gain per day was weakly and negatively related to days between meets, baseline strength and number of competitions.
- 검증 메모: Verified through Crossref (the DOI page failed). The SD is very large (0.44 kg per day). Note that 55 kg per year of TOTAL is roughly 18 kg per year per lift for the average competitor, well above the 3-10 kg per year band used elsewhere in these findings.
- REF5 관련성: Confirms that progression slows as strength rises, which is relevant to how often REF5's 2.5 kg steps can be earned.

The argument should rest on high-bar kinematics (more knee flexion) plus the athlete's own symptoms, not on EMG or on quadriceps-tendinopathy prognosis data. Keeping symptom monitoring outside the judgment system remains reasonable.
- 'DRUG-TESTED' LABEL AND PERCENTILE BAND. Of the competition sources, only van den Hoek 2024 is drug-tested (confirmed through a secondary source). Naylor 2023 pools all raw results since 1964 across federations, and its class means (155 and 170 kg) are not in the abstract and are unverified. FitnessVolt gives tier ranges whose boundary meaning is unstated, and its '90th = 215 kg' and '~59,000 results' do not appear on the page. The '60th-85th percentile of drug-tested specialists' band should be anchored on Latella 2019 (between the local and international 74 kg means) and the van den Hoek 90th percentile (2.83x), not on FitnessVolt or Naylor point estimates. van den Hoek 2026 (norms drifting upward) could not be confirmed to exist and should not be cited.
- LONG-TERM PROGRESSION DETAILS. For Latella 2024, only 'about 7.5-12.5% in year 1, up to about 20% at 10 years' and 'females progressed faster' are verified. The sample size, 'squat progressed fastest', 'lighter lifters faster', drug-tested status and 'survivorship bias unlikely' are unverified, so 'tested competitors gain only 12.5-20% over 10 years' should become 'powerlifters gain up to about 20% over 10 years'. Steele 2023's numbers (30-50% in year 1, plateau after 1-2 years, 50-60% at 6 years) are unverified. Nuckols 2022, which describes the same dataset, frames the curve as continued logarithmic slowing with 'always gains to be made', not a plateau. The Nuckols 2018 0.576% per month is a mean, not a median.
- SIZE OF THE CEILING-VERSUS-BIOLOGY GAP. The '4-15x' multiplier holds only against the 3-10 kg per year band, which comes mainly from Nuckols' percentage rates. Against confirmed competitor data (Latella 2020: average male total +0.15 kg per day, about 55 kg per year across three lifts, about 18 kg per lift; Latella 2022: squat best-year gains of 20.2-25.4 kg per year), the REF5 ceiling (about 38-55 kg per year REF) is only about 1.5-3x higher. The conclusion that the ceiling is not binding still holds, but the margin is smaller than stated.
- VOLUME FLATTENING. Pelland's '~4 sets' marks where the MARGINAL gain per added set falls below the smallest detectable effect, not where strength gains stop. The model still predicts about +0.21% per extra weekly set. The claim that 'more volume would yield little extra strength' should be softened to 'diminishing but non-zero returns'. The Sports Medicine 2026 journal citation is unverified; cite the preprint unless it is checked.
- BODYWEIGHT SCALING COST OF THE CUT. Markovic and Sekulic 2006 is cross-sectional, between-class scaling of elite totals (and the allometric model favoured middleweights). Using it to predict an individual's squat after weight loss is an extrapolation. The figure '103 kg at 75 kg becomes about 98 kg at 70 kg' assumes the whole 5 kg is lost proportionally, not merely 'any lean mass'. If the weight lost is mostly fat (as Garthe suggests is possible at slow rates), the absolute-squat stall may be much smaller than implied.
- NO-1RM-TEST COST, minor. Nuzzo 2024 supports between-person variation in reps-%1RM but says nothing about within-person 'drift over time'. That part is extrapolation.
- UNCERTAINTY LIST, minor. 'Barbell Medicine notes there is no direct study of belt effects on squat 1RM' is not supported: the cited Barbell Medicine page does not mention belts. The 50-60% heritability figure cites a reference that is missing from the article's list.

## REF5 함의: 조사 단계 원안

- FEASIBILITY OF G1 (rarity), extrapolated from direct normative data. A 175 kg high-bar squat at about 70 kg is 2.5x bodyweight. Small studies (Gonzalez 2025, Murawa 2020) and coaching sources (Nuckols, 5-10%) put low-bar about 5-10% above high-bar. So 175 kg high-bar is roughly a 184-193 kg competition-style squat (about 2.6-2.75x bodyweight) before any belt or peaking effect. Against drug-tested raw competitors (van den Hoek 2024: 90th percentile 2.83x for men 18-35; FitnessVolt 74 kg class median 172.5 and 75th percentile 195; Naylor 2023 class means 155/170 kg), that is roughly the 60th-85th percentile of people who specialise in powerlifting. Among app-logging gym users (StrengthLog 95th percentile at 70 kg = 157 kg; Strength Level about 90th percentile), it is top 5-10%. In the trained general population it is likely top 1-3% (my extrapolation). Verdict: rare, but achievable by some drug-free lifters. It is an upper-quartile specialist level, not a guaranteed outcome of good programming.
- FEASIBILITY OF G1 (timeline), extrapolation. The gap is +72 kg (+70%) from about 103 kg at 1.37x bodyweight, while losing about 5 kg. Tested competitors gain only about 12.5-20% over 10 years after their first meet (Latella 2023). Median rates fall from about 9% per year to about 3% per year within about 2 years (Nuckols 2018). Constant-rate arithmetic gives 5.6 years at 10% per year, 7.8 at 7%, 10.9 at 5% and 17.9 at 3%. With realistic decaying rates, many trajectories level off below 175 (for example 7% per year decaying 15% per year levels off near 163 kg). Mitigating factor: 1.37x bodyweight is about the 10th percentile of competitors and below what multi-year lifters usually reach. The one-year plateau therefore more plausibly reflects the athlete's history of never combining frequency, proximity and recoverable volume (their own diagnosis) than a genetic ceiling, so the first 1-2 years may run faster than competitor averages. My subjective expectation (opinion): about 115-125 kg within 12-18 months, about 140 kg (2.0x at 70 kg) in about 3-5 years, and 175 kg in about 7-15 years if the individual ceiling allows it. Probability is uncertain and plausibly below 50%. This concerns the §20 operating profile (a goal, not a protocol rule). Evidence supports treating 175 kg as a long-horizon direction with intermediate milestones, not as a dated target.
- REF5's SQ PROGRESSION CEILING IS NOT THE BINDING CONSTRAINT (rule: SQ +2.5 kg per 6 valid hard exposures with FAIL 0 and HOLD <= 1, plus the 48 h / 168 h hard selector). At the observed 1.75 hard starts per week, a window takes about 3.4 weeks, so H3 can rise at most about 38 kg per year (about 48 kg per year in control-REF terms, x104/82.5). The selector's absolute maximum (2 hard starts per 168 h) gives about 43 kg per year H3, or about 55 kg REF. Realistic biological rates at this level are about 3-10 kg per year of 1RM (Nuckols 2018; Latella 2020/2022/2023). Even competitive lifters' best years are about 20-25 kg per year (Latella 2022). The ceiling exceeds realistic adaptation by about 4-15x. Evidence supports that the cap does not limit G1. Evidence is silent on whether 6 exposures is the optimal window length.
- CURRENT ALL-INCREASE RECORD = CATCH-UP PHASE, NOT ADAPTATION (rule: REF5 starting standards from e1RM, §5.3 and §21 evaluation gate). H3 started at about 78-80% and is now 85 kg (about 82.5% of a ~103 kg 1RM). The athlete's prior best was 92.5x3 at RPE 9-10. From reps-%1RM relationships (Nuzzo 2023), a 3x3 with no obvious slowdown is plausible up to about 87.5-90 kg (extrapolation). The program will probably reach the athlete's true capacity within about 1-3 more SQ windows (roughly by Dec 2026 to Feb 2027 if training is regular). After that, the progression rate equals the biological rate. The same holds for PULL (87.5 kg total started at about 80% of a 110 kg total). Evidence supports pre-registering, at the first evaluation gate, that windows closed during catch-up carry no information about adaptation rate or about the 175 kg timeline.
- THE REAL MECHANICAL CONSTRAINT IS STEP SIZE VERSUS ADAPTATION PER WINDOW (rules: 2.5 kg grid; two consecutive maintain windows lead to stagnation micro, then reassessment, then a decrease; §22.4 items 3-4). Once at capacity, a +2.5 kg H3 step (about 2.9% of H3, about 3.1 kg of REF) needs about 4-12 months of real adaptation at 3-7% per year. That is about 5-15 SQ windows. The stagnation path, however, fires after about 3 windows (about 2.5-3 months). Expected steady state (inference, not tested): a sawtooth of increase, maintain, maintain, reassess, decrease, re-increase, whose average tracks true capacity. The pre-registered reading (§21.1: low success rate is first read as insufficient stimulus or recovery) will then raise false alarms, because the low success rate is simply the normal intermediate rate. Without relaxing FAIL 0 / HOLD <= 1 / the volume veto, the evidence supports one interpretive addition (an interpretation rule, not a protocol change): pre-register an expected long-run benchmark, for example control REF +3 to +10 kg per year over 3 or more windows or about 6 months. Only a REF trend clearly below that benchmark, not the stagnation sawtooth itself, should trigger the §23.1 stimulus/recovery audit. The same applies more strongly to OHP, where one 2.5 kg step is about 7.7% of the standard (§22.4 item 5).
- SQUAT DOSE AND FREQUENCY ARE PROBABLY NOT THE LIMITER (rules: SQ H3/H2/V prescriptions and the weekly set table: 6/9/11 SQ sets at 2/3/4 sessions per week). About 5 hard sets per week at about 79-87% plus V sets meets or exceeds the minimum effective dose for powerlifters (Androulakis-Korakakis 2021: 3-6 sets of 1-5 reps above 80%). Strength dose-response flattens after about 4 sets per week and with frequency (Pelland 2024/2026). Frequency acts mainly through volume (Grgic 2018). Evidence supports the current SQ dose and the 2-per-168 h hard cap for the next 1-2 years. It is silent on what dose sustains multi-year progress toward 2.5x bodyweight. Coaching norms for intermediate powerlifters often run higher (opinion).
- BODYWEIGHT CUT VERSUS G1 VERSUS G6 (§20 operating profile: 0.25% per week, stop the cut after an SQ decrease). A 0.25% per week cut is far below rates that harmed strength or lean mass in an RCT and a meta-analysis (Garthe 2011: 0.7% per week still raised lean mass; Murphy and Koehler 2022: strength not significantly impaired, lean mass harmed at about 500 kcal/day or more). Supported. Cost under 2/3 allometric scaling (Markovic and Sekulic 2006): if any lean mass is lost, 103 kg at 75 kg corresponds to about 98 kg at 70 kg, so absolute squat progress may stall for the roughly 6-7 months the cut takes (5 kg at 0.25% per week is about 27 weeks). The same cut raises the athlete's pull total-to-bodyweight ratio from 1.47x to about 1.57x at an unchanged total, worth roughly 7-8 kg of weighted pull-up for G6. The two goals pull bodyweight in opposite directions. The §20 'stop the cut on an SQ decrease' rule is consistent with the evidence.
- BODYWEIGHT DATA QUALITY IS A PREREQUISITE FOR EVALUATING EITHER GOAL (rules: PULL tracked as total load = bodyweight + added, §22.4 item 6; §21 KPI control REF divided by 7-day mean bodyweight). The logged 75 kg on every session is probably a default. If real bodyweight falls toward 70 kg while 75 is logged, the engine prescribes added load as total minus 75. The real total would then be up to 5 kg lighter than intended, giving easier PULL exposures, inflated PASS outcomes and REF inflation. That is exactly the failure mode the user wants to avoid. The squat KPI (REF/bodyweight) would also be wrong. The literature is silent here (this is logic, not evidence), but this is the highest-leverage measurement fix. It is operational, not a protocol change.
- G6 FEASIBILITY (one-arm chin-up), extrapolation from coaching sources and sparse data. The athlete's 1RM total of 110 kg at 75 kg is 1.47x bodyweight (+47%). Coaching thresholds are about 1.67x total (Willoughby's 2/3-bodyweight rule) and a 3RM at +60% bodyweight (JASC 2019, implying a 1RM around +65-70%). Advanced climbers at about 1.63-1.67x total mostly could NOT do a one-arm pull-up (Sas-Nowosielski 2022: 2 of 14). One-arm conversions are crude because the bilateral deficit is variable (Skarabot 2016). Gap: about +10-18 kg on the weighted pull-up total at 75 kg bodyweight, or about +2-9 kg at 70 kg if the total is held. Opinion: a one-arm chin-up (supinated, the stated first target) is plausible in about 6-18 months, mainly as the cut lands and if the elbow tolerates the work. A strict pronated one-arm pull-up is longer and less certain. There is no peer-reviewed data on time-to-achievement.
- OAP LADDER SPEED IS NOT BINDING; STRENGTH AND TISSUE TOLERANCE ARE (rules: §7.5 promotion after 3 consecutive PASS, demotion after 2 consecutive FAIL, 6 rungs, at least 2 weeks per rung guideline). From rung 1 to rung 6 takes at least 15 PASS exposures per arm. At the observed ~1.1 OAP sessions per week, that is at least about 14 weeks. The 2-weeks-per-rung guideline alone is at least 10 weeks. The observed record (FAIL, demotion to rung 1, then HOLD/PASS) fits an athlete about 10-20% short of a one-arm strength threshold. The binding inputs for G6 are therefore PULL-focus progression (the weighted pull-up total), bodyweight, and elbow tolerance with climbing. Evidence supports keeping OAP independent of kg progression. It is silent on whether hand-position rungs map onto strength in any equal-interval way (§22.4 item 11 already notes they do not).
- PULL CEILING AND THE v1.4 VOLUME TRADE (rules: PULL +2.5 kg per 4 focus exposures; v1.4 cut PULL from about 8 to about 6 weekly sets, §22.4 item 9). The ceiling is about 36-43 kg per year of total load, many times any plausible weighted pull-up gain at this level. Competitive bench gains of 10.5-12.8 kg per year at their best (Latella 2022) are the closest upper-body analogue, so pull-up gains are probably lower (extrapolation). Not binding. Because strength dose-response flattens beyond about 4 sets per week (Pelland), losing about 2 low-intensity PULL sets probably costs little 1RM pulling strength. Evidence is silent on hypertrophy or skill transfer to OAP. Evidence mildly supports the v1.4 trade for G6.
- NO-1RM-TEST CONSTRAINT: COST (C9, C10, no AMRAP; §20 says goal attainment cannot be confirmed from control REF). The KPI the user chose (squat e1RM / bodyweight) cannot be measured inside REF5. Control REF is a fixed multiple of H3 (x104/82.5), and the true reps-%1RM mapping varies between people and drifts over time (Nuzzo 2023). Evidence supports §22.4 item 8. Within the user's constraints, REF trend plus correctly measured bodyweight is the best available proxy, but it can neither confirm nor rule out reaching 175 kg. This is a described cost, not a recommendation to test.
- KNEE AS A CANDIDATE BINDING CONSTRAINT (handled outside the protocol; auto-micro is not rehab). High-bar puts more demand on knee flexion and the quadriceps than low-bar (Glassbrook 2017). Quadriceps tendinopathy is associated with squatting pain and early sport retirement in small cohorts (King 2019, low quality). Pushing high-bar loads toward 2.5x bodyweight on an already symptomatic suprapatellar tendon is a plausible long-term limiter, more so than any REF5 rule. Evidence is weak but consistent with keeping symptom monitoring (pain 0-3/10) outside the judgment system, as the user does.

## 불확실성

- The athlete's true squat 1RM is unknown. About 103 kg is inferred from 92.5x3 at RPE 9-10, so it is uncertain by roughly +/-3-5 kg. The catch-up projection (1-3 more windows) depends on it.
- The high-bar to low-bar gap is individual. The 5-10% figure rests on two small crossover studies (n=12 and n=19; about 6-7%) plus coaching opinion. Belt, knee sleeves, peaking and competition depth standards add an unquantified further gap, and Barbell Medicine notes there is no direct study of belt effects on squat 1RM.
- Competition percentiles describe self-selected specialists (mostly low-bar, belted, peaked, depth judged only to parallel). App datasets (StrengthLog, Strength Level) are self-reported, often estimated, and depth is unverified. Neither describes high-bar gym squats directly. FitnessVolt and Liftvault methods (best per lifter, federation, years) are not fully stated.
- Long-term progression data (Latella 2020/2022/2023, Nuckols 2018/2022) come from competitors or a machine-training gym chain with unknown programs. They can overstate (dropout of non-progressors) or understate (suboptimal training) what a well-structured program achieves for a lifter whose history may be under-realized. Individual variance is large: the sex, age and strength models explain only about 11% of variance.
- Pelland et al. dose-response numbers are taken from the 2024 preprint. The published Sports Medicine 2026 version (vol. 56, pp. 481-505, per the SPONET index) could not be read directly, and the study pool has low average volume and short durations, so extrapolating to multi-year advanced training is uncertain.
- I found no peer-reviewed study linking weighted pull-up 1RM to achieving a one-arm chin-up or pull-up, and none on how long it takes. The 2/3-bodyweight rule and the JASC +60% bodyweight 3RM criterion are practitioner heuristics. Sas-Nowosielski 2022 is a single small climbing sample where one-arm grip and assistance details are limited.
- The real bodyweight trend is unknown because every session logs 75 kg. This undermines both the REF/bodyweight squat KPI and the PULL total-load arithmetic, and changes the G6 gap estimate by up to about 7-8 kg.
- The size of genetic and individual ceilings cannot be known in advance. The cited 50-60% heritability of baseline strength is reported in a coaching article and was not verified against the primary twin studies.
- The predicted 'stagnation sawtooth' at intermediate rates comes from mapping literature rates onto REF5 rules. It has not been observed in this athlete's data yet. Day-to-day readiness variance and the HOLD <= 1 allowance could make increases happen more often than the simple calculation suggests.
- The knee (undiagnosed suprapatellar pain) and the elbow (heavy pulling plus OAP plus climbing) could dominate both timelines, but the evidence on quadriceps tendinopathy is low quality and climbing-elbow incidence data were not quantified here.
