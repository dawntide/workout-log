# 04. 회복·감량·병행 운동 (`recovery-deficit-concurrent`)

> 2026-10-15 REF5 근거 평가 워크플로의 원자료다. 본문은 조사·검증 에이전트가 쓴 영어 원문 그대로다. 상태: confirmed(원문 확인), corrected(출처는 있으나 서지·수치를 고침, `검증 결과`를 쓸 것), unverifiable(확인 불가, 재확인 전 근거로 쓰지 말 것), misattributed(저자 귀속 오류).

## 조사 범위

RECOVERY CONTEXT: energy deficit and recomposition, protein, sleep and stress, concurrent climbing, exercise order, recovery time between sessions (48–168 h gates, MICRO valve, deloads)

## 출처 (35건: confirmed 22, corrected 12, unverifiable 1)

### RC-01 · confirmed

**Murphy C, Koehler K. (2022) Energy deficiency impairs resistance training gains in lean mass but not strength: A meta-analysis and meta-regression. Scand J Med Sci Sports 32(1):125-137. doi:10.1111/sms.14075**

- meta-analysis · 2022 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34623696/
- 조사 요약: Training in an energy deficit impaired lean-mass gains (ES -0.57, p=0.02) but not strength gains (ES -0.31, p=0.28; within-group strength ES 0.84 in the deficit vs 0.81 in the control). Meta-regression: each ~1000 kcal/d of deficit reduced the lean-mass ES by ~0.31 (intercept 0.16), so a deficit of about 500 kcal/d was estimated to abolish lean-mass gain. The strength analysis rests on few studies (k about 5).
- 검증 결과: Abstract (Europe PMC record) confirms: RCTs of at least 3 weeks; lean mass RT+ED vs RT+CON ES -0.57 (p=0.02); strength ES -0.31 (p=0.28); within-group lean mass ES -0.11 (ED) vs +0.20 (CON); within-group strength ES 0.84 (ED) vs 0.81 (CON). Meta-regression: a deficit of about 500 kcal/day prevented lean-mass gains, and the authors advise avoiding deficits above 500 kcal/day.
- 검증 메모: The bibliographic details and all abstract numbers match. The regression slope (about -0.31 ES per 1000 kcal/d), the intercept (0.16) and k≈5 for strength are NOT in the abstract. The full text (Wiley OA PDF, TUM repository) could not be fetched, so those three numbers are unverified. They are internally consistent with the ~500 kcal/d zero-crossing, but should not be quoted as checked.
- REF5 관련성: §20 / §23.1 recomposition at 0.25%/wk is about 0.19 kg/wk at 75 kg, roughly a 200 kcal/d deficit. That is well under the ~500 kcal/d threshold, so strength progression can be expected. Lean-mass accrual, which the long-run SQ target (G1) partly depends on, is attenuated rather than abolished. This last point is an extrapolation from the regression line.
- 조사 당시 표기: Murphy C, Koehler K. (2022) Energy deficiency impairs resistance training gains in lean mass but not strength: A meta-analysis and meta-regression. Scand J Med Sci Sports 32(1):125-137

### RC-02 · confirmed

**Garthe I, Raastad T, Refsnes PE, Koivisto A, Sundgot-Borgen J. (2011) Effect of two different weight-loss rates on body composition and strength and power-related performance in elite athletes. Int J Sport Nutr Exerc Metab 21(2):97-104**

- RCT · 2011 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21558571/
- 조사 요약: 24 elite athletes did resistance training while losing weight. The slow arm targeted 0.7% BW/wk; the fast arm targeted 1.4%/wk and achieved about 1.0%/wk. The slow arm gained about 2.1% lean body mass and improved 1RM in all tested lifts. The fast arm kept lean mass unchanged and improved only squat 1RM.
- 검증 결과: 24 elite athletes (slow-rate arm n=13, fast-rate arm n=11) did 4 resistance sessions/wk. Targets were 0.7%/wk and 1.4%/wk; achieved rates were 0.7±0.8%/wk and 1.0±0.4%/wk. The slow arm took 8.5 wk and the fast arm 5.3 wk, and both lost about 5.5% of body weight. Lean body mass rose 2.1±0.4% in the slow arm and was unchanged (-0.2±0.7%) in the fast arm (p<.01). Authors' conclusion: aim for about 0.7%/wk to gain lean mass and increase 1RM while losing weight.
- 검증 메모: The abstract gives no per-lift 1RM results. The claims that the slow arm improved 1RM in all tested lifts and the fast arm only in squat could not be checked against the abstract; the full text is paywalled. Treat them as unverified.
- REF5 관련성: Direct support for a slow loss rate. REF5's 0.25%/wk is about a third of Garthe's slow arm, so the cost to strength should be small. The study also supports §20's rule to stop the cut on SQ stagnation or an immediate decrease.

### RC-03 · confirmed

**Helms ER, Zinn C, Rowlands DS, Brown SR. (2014) A systematic review of dietary protein during caloric restriction in resistance trained lean athletes: a case for higher intakes. Int J Sport Nutr Exerc Metab 24(2):127-138**

- review · 2014 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24092765/
- 조사 요약: Systematic review of 6 studies. For lean, resistance-trained people in a caloric deficit, protein needs are likely 2.3-3.1 g/kg of fat-free mass, rising with the severity of the deficit and with leanness.
- 검증 결과: Systematic review of 6 studies (13 study groups). Protein needs for energy-restricted, resistance-trained athletes are likely 2.3-3.1 g/kg of FFM, rising with the severity of the caloric restriction and with leanness.
- REF5 관련성: §23.1 counts days below 1.6 g/kg as the protein flag. During a deficit, this evidence puts the useful range above that floor. That is an argument about the personal nutrition target, not about the REF5 protocol.

### RC-04 · corrected

**Refalo MC, Trexler ET, Helms ER. Effect of Dietary Protein on Fat-Free Mass in Energy Restricted, Resistance-Trained Individuals: An Updated Systematic Review With Meta-Regression. Strength Cond J (published online 22 Jan 2025). doi:10.1519/SSC.0000000000000888**

- meta-analysis · 2026 · https://doi.org/10.1519/SSC.0000000000000888
- 조사 요약: Bayesian meta-regression of 29 studies. In energy-restricted, resistance-trained people, fat-free mass retention rose linearly with protein intake (probability >97%; beta about 0.06 per g/kg FFM). The effect was stronger in men, in leaner people and in interventions longer than 4 weeks. The authors call it exploratory.
- 검증 결과: Crossref record and abstract: 29 of 916 retrieved studies were analysed with Bayesian meta-regression. There is a >97% probability of a linear dose-response between protein intake and favourable FFM change. Per kg FFM: β=0.06 (95% HDI 0.01-0.12). Per kg body mass: β=0.07 (95% HDI -0.01 to 0.14), so the interval includes zero. The relationship was stronger when protein was expressed per kg FFM, in interventions longer than 4 weeks, in men, and at lower baseline body fat. The authors call the findings exploratory because of heterogeneity.
- 검증 메모: Corrections: the actual title is 'An Updated Systematic Review With Meta-Regression', not 'a Bayesian meta-regression'. It was first published online in January 2025. The '2026 48(4):398-412' issue details could not be verified; they may be the later print issue, but cite the online 2025 date unless confirmed. The LWW URL could not be fetched. Also, the per-body-mass slope is not credibly non-zero, so any target expressed in g/kg BW is an extrapolation from the per-FFM result.
- REF5 관련성: This is the newest direct evidence that, during a cut, more protein than the 1.6 g/kg plateau seen in energy balance helps retain muscle. It supports moving the §23.1 audit threshold, or the personal target, toward ≥2.0-2.2 g/kg BW while the cut lasts.
- 조사 당시 표기: Refalo MC, Trexler ET, Helms ER. (2026) Effect of dietary protein on fat-free mass in energy-restricted, resistance-trained individuals: a Bayesian meta-regression. Strength Cond J 48(4):398-412. doi:10.1519/SSC.0000000000000888
- 조사 당시 URL: https://journals.lww.com/nsca-scj/fulltext/9900/effect_of_dietary_protein_on_fat_free_mass_in.179.aspx

### RC-05 · confirmed

**Roth C, Schoenfeld BJ, Behringer M. (2022) Lean mass sparing in resistance-trained athletes during caloric restriction: the role of resistance training volume. Eur J Appl Physiol 122:1129-1151**

- review · 2022 · https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00421-022-04896-5
- 조사 요약: Review of 15 studies. Cutting resistance-training volume during caloric restriction may worsen lean-mass sparing, so volume should probably be maintained. The authors judge the direct evidence insufficient.
- 검증 결과: Invited review with a PRISMA-guided search; 15 studies in the qualitative analysis. The authors state that evidence is insufficient to conclude that higher RT volume spares more lean mass during caloric restriction, largely because only 4 of 15 studies reported enough to compute volume. Studies that increased volume during restriction showed less lean-mass loss than studies that reduced it, which is tentative. High volume (≥10 sets/muscle/wk) was linked to little or no lean-mass loss, mainly in women.
- 검증 메모: The gist matches. The authors lean toward maintaining or increasing volume, but explicitly label the evidence insufficient.
- REF5 관련성: Supports REF5 keeping prescribed volume unchanged during the cut, with no diet-triggered volume reduction. It also argues against using MICRO as a habit during the cut rather than as a reactive valve. This is an extrapolation.

### RC-06 · confirmed

**Morton RW, Murphy KT, McKellar SR, et al. (2018) A systematic review, meta-analysis and meta-regression of the effect of protein supplementation on resistance training-induced gains in muscle mass and strength in healthy adults. Br J Sports Med 52(6):376-384**

- meta-analysis · 2018 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/
- 조사 요약: 49 RCTs (n=1863). Protein supplementation added about 2.49 kg to 1RM gains and about 0.30 kg to fat-free mass. Gains in fat-free mass plateaued at about 1.62 g/kg/d (95% CI upper bound about 2.2 g/kg/d). Most participants were in energy balance.
- 검증 결과: 49 RCTs, n=1863, RT for at least 6 weeks. Protein supplementation increased 1RM by 2.49 kg (95% CI 0.64-4.33) and FFM by 0.30 kg (0.09-0.52). FFM gains plateaued at a total protein intake of 1.62 g/kg/d (about 1.6 g/kg/d).
- 검증 메모: The CI upper bound of about 2.2 g/kg/d is from the full text (it is widely cited as 1.03-2.20) and is not in the abstract. 'Most participants in energy balance' is not stated in the abstract and was not verified.
- REF5 관련성: This is where the 1.6 g/kg floor in §20 / §23.1 comes from. The plateau was measured mostly in energy balance, so it is the right floor for maintenance and possibly too low for the cut phase.

### RC-07 · confirmed

**Nunes EA, Colenso-Semple L, McKellar SR, et al. (2022) Systematic review and meta-analysis of protein intake to support muscle mass and function in healthy adults. J Cachexia Sarcopenia Muscle 13(2):795-810**

- meta-analysis · 2022 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35187864/
- 조사 요약: 74 RCTs. In adults under 65, intakes ≥1.6 g/kg/d with resistance training gave small gains in lean body mass (SMD about 0.22) and in lower-body strength (SMD about 0.40, low certainty), and a smaller effect on bench press (SMD about 0.18).
- 검증 결과: 74 RCTs. With resistance exercise, higher protein increased lean body mass (SMD 0.22, 95% CI 0.14-0.30, moderate certainty); in adults under 65 the effect appeared at ≥1.6 g/kg/d. Lower-body strength at ≥1.6 g/kg/d: SMD 0.40 (19 studies, low certainty; the printed CI 0.09-0.35 does not contain the point estimate and is likely a typo). Bench press in adults under 65: SMD 0.18 (0.03-0.33, low certainty).
- 검증 메모: Cite the lower-body SMD with the CI-typo caveat.
- REF5 관련성: Supports keeping the 1.6 g/kg floor in §20 / §23.1 for strength. The effect is small, so protein is a secondary lever compared with sleep and energy balance.

### RC-08 · unverifiable

- 검증 메모: The specific figures (protein 2.6-3.5 g/kg FFM, ≥3 sessions/wk) are not in the abstract, and the LWW full text could not be fetched. Treat the numbers as unverified.
- REF5 관련성: Supports aiming for recomposition with strength progression under §20. Its emphasis on sleep and protein lines up with the §23.1 recovery audit items. Coaching and review-level evidence.

### RC-09 · confirmed

**Mountjoy M, Ackerman KE, Bailey DM, et al. (2023) 2023 International Olympic Committee's (IOC) consensus statement on Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs). Br J Sports Med 57(17):1073-1097**

- consensus · 2023 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37752011/
- 조사 요약: Problematic low energy availability also affects males. It can impair training response, performance, recovery and injury resistance. The consensus separates adaptable (brief, mild) from problematic (sustained or severe) low energy availability.
- 검증 결과: IOC consensus that adds data on low energy availability in males. It introduces a physiological model in which LEA exposure is 'problematic or adaptable', and REDs is defined as a syndrome of impaired health and performance caused by LEA. It also presents REDs CAT2.
- 검증 메모: The abstract does not detail training-response effects; those claims rest on the full text, which is consistent with the consensus.
- REF5 관련성: The conservative 0.25%/wk rate in §20 should keep the user in the adaptable range. Combining a deficit, climbing energy cost and self-reported poor recovery is the scenario in which energy availability should be checked first in the §23.1 audit.

### RC-10 · corrected

**Craven J, McCartney D, Desbrow B, Sabapathy S, Bellinger P, Roberts L, Irwin C. (2022) Effects of Acute Sleep Loss on Physical Performance: A Systematic and Meta-Analytical Review. Sports Med 52(11):2669-2690**

- meta-analysis · 2022 · https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-022-01706-y
- 조사 요약: 69 publications. Acute sleep loss (≤6 h) reduced performance by about 7.56% on average, including strength. Effects came mainly from total deprivation and late-night restriction, at about 0.4% per extra hour awake. Afternoon performance was consistently impaired; morning performance was largely unaffected.
- 검증 결과: 69 publications (77 studies, n=959, 89% male). Mean performance change across ALL tasks was -7.56%. Performance fell about 0.4% per hour awake (significant for restriction and late restriction). Deprivation and late restriction had the most consistent effects; early restriction did not. PM tasks were more affected and AM tasks largely unaffected (except endurance). STRENGTH specifically: mean -2.85% (25 studies); significant only for total sleep deprivation (-3.0%); lower-body strength was affected but upper-body strength was not.
- 검증 메모: The -7.56% figure is all-task and endurance-weighted, not strength. For REF5's lifts the relevant figure is about -3%, and only after total deprivation. The title is 'Systematic and Meta-Analytical Review', not 'Systematic Review and Meta-Analytical Review'. PMID 35708888 was confirmed via OpenAlex.
- REF5 관련성: A short-sleep night before a session can lower the outcome of the judged sets. REF5 has no sleep input, so this shows up as HOLD/FAIL noise in the judgment windows. That is the first hypothesis the user pre-registered, and the reason the §23.1 audit starts with sleep.
- 조사 당시 표기: Craven J, McCartney D, Desbrow B, et al. (2022) Effects of Acute Sleep Loss on Physical Performance: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analytical Review. Sports Med 52(11):2669-2690
- 조사 당시 URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35708888/

### RC-11 · confirmed

**Easow J, Bommasamudram T, Munnilari M, et al. (2025) Implications of sleep loss or sleep deprivation on muscle strength: a systematic review. Sleep Breath 29(4):242**

- review · 2025 · https://doi.org/10.1007/s11325-025-03413-0
- 조사 요약: Systematic review of 13 studies. Findings were mixed: some studies showed lower strength, power and endurance after sleep deprivation; others showed no change after a single night. Fatigue and impaired neuromuscular function were common.
- 검증 결과: 13 studies. Some found that sleep deprivation reduced strength; others found no significant difference after a single night. Acute and chronic sleep deprivation significantly reduced some measures of strength, power and muscular endurance. Impaired neuromuscular function and increased fatigue were common.

### RC-12 · confirmed

**Knowles OE, Drinkwater EJ, Urwin CS, Lamon S, Aisbett B. (2018) Inadequate sleep and muscle strength: Implications for resistance training. J Sci Med Sport 21(9):959-968**

- review · 2018 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29422383/
- 조사 요약: Systematic review of 17 studies. One night of total deprivation had little effect on maximal strength. Several consecutive nights of partial restriction reduced force in multi-joint compound lifts but not single-joint movements.
- 검증 결과: Systematic review of 17 studies (moderate or weak quality). Sleep deprivation had little effect on muscle strength during resistance exercise. Consecutive nights of sleep restriction could reduce force output in multi-joint but not single-joint movements. Hormonal results conflicted. Group training, caffeine and training before prolonged wakefulness were suggested as mitigations.
- REF5 관련성: All REF5 judged lifts are multi-joint compounds, the category most sensitive to chronic restriction. Supports §23.1's 4-week sleep look-back and the user's pre-registered reading that recovery comes before blaming the program.

### RC-13 · corrected

**Reilly T, Piercy M. (1994) The effect of partial sleep deprivation on weight-lifting performance. Ergonomics 37(1):107-115. PMID 8112265**

- RCT · 1994 · https://doi.org/10.1080/00140139408963628
- 조사 요약: Crossover study, n=8, 3 h sleep per night for 3 nights. Maximal bench press, leg press and deadlift declined. Submaximal lifts done for 20 reps declined from the 2nd night onward, and more than maximal lifts.
- 검증 결과: Counterbalanced crossover with 8 men aged 18-24, 3 h sleep/night for 3 nights versus normal sleep, tested in the evenings. Tasks were biceps curl, bench press, leg press and deadlift. Maximal bench press, leg press and deadlift fell significantly; maximal biceps curl did not. The submaximal task was the load lifted for 20 reps at a fixed perceived-exertion rating. It declined in all four tasks, significantly after the 2nd night.
- 검증 메모: Corrections: (1) the submaximal measure was an RPE-anchored load for 20 reps, not reps completed at a fixed load. It maps onto REF5's fixed-load judged sets only indirectly. (2) The abstract does not say submaximal declined MORE than maximal; it says submaximal declined across all 4 tasks, including curl, where maximal did not. The abstract was verified via OpenAlex. The bisp-surf URL was not fetchable, so use the DOI.
- REF5 관련성: REF5 judges success at fixed submaximal loads for multiple reps (H3/H2/V, BP 2×5), the format this study found most sleep-sensitive. Short sleep therefore maps directly onto HOLD/FAIL risk. Old and small study, but mechanistically aligned with Knowles 2018.
- 조사 당시 표기: Reilly T, Piercy M. (1994) The effect of partial sleep deprivation on weight-lifting performance. Ergonomics 37(1):107-115
- 조사 당시 URL: https://www.bisp-surf.de/Record/PU199502072760

### RC-14 · corrected

**Lamon S, Morabito A, Arentson-Lantz E, et al. (2021) The effect of acute sleep deprivation on skeletal muscle protein synthesis and the hormonal environment. Physiol Rep 9(1):e14660**

- RCT · 2021 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7785053/
- 조사 요약: Crossover study, n=13. One night without sleep reduced postprandial muscle protein synthesis by about 18%, raised cortisol by about 21% and lowered testosterone by about 24% (in men).
- 검증 결과: Randomised crossover, n=13 healthy young adults (7 male, 6 female). One night of total sleep deprivation reduced postprandial MPS by 18% (p=.040), raised the cortisol AUC by 21% and lowered the testosterone AUC by 24% (p=.029). These effects are for the pooled mixed-sex sample. Males all showed numerical MPS and testosterone decreases, but sex effects were not formally tested.
- 검증 메모: The '(in men)' qualifier is not how the paper reports the testosterone result; the 24% figure is for the pooled sample. This is a single night of TOTAL deprivation, not short sleep.
- REF5 관련성: Mechanistic support that poor sleep slows adaptation as well as performance. Combined with a deficit (Murphy 2022), it compounds the lean-mass cost. Relevant to §20 and §23.1.

### RC-15 · confirmed

**Stults-Kolehmainen MA, Bartholomew JB, Sinha R. (2014) Chronic psychological stress impairs recovery of muscular function and somatic sensations over a 96-hour period. J Strength Cond Res 28(7):2007-2017**

- cohort · 2014 · https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000000335
- 조사 요약: n=31 after strenuous leg-press work. Higher chronic life stress and perceived stress predicted slower 96 h recovery of maximal isometric force, perceived energy and fatigue, and soreness.
- 검증 결과: n=31 undergraduates; 10RM leg-press test followed by 6 sets at 80-100% 10RM. Maximal isometric force, perceived energy, fatigue and soreness were measured about every 24 h for 96 h. Life-event stress moderated recovery of MIF, energy, fatigue and soreness. Perceived stress moderated MIF and energy recovery but NOT fatigue or soreness. Higher stress meant worse recovery.
- 검증 메모: Verified via OpenAlex abstract (PMID 24343323). Minor nuance: perceived stress did not predict fatigue or soreness.
- REF5 관련성: §23.1 audits sleep, protein and loss rate but not psychological stress. This evidence suggests stress is an independent recovery variable worth logging in the audit; adding it changes no protocol.
- 조사 당시 URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24343323/

### RC-16 · confirmed

**Bartholomew JB, Stults-Kolehmainen MA, Elrod CC, Todd JS. (2008) Strength gains after resistance training: the effect of stressful, negative life events. J Strength Cond Res 22(4):1215-1221**

- cohort · 2008 · https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0b013e318173d0bf
- 조사 요약: n=135 over 12 weeks. People with fewer stressful life events gained more strength than the high-stress group (squat about +27% vs +23%; bench about +15% vs +12%).
- 검증 결과: n=135 undergraduates in weight-training classes, 12 weeks at 2×/wk, split at the median of life-event stress. Low-stress participants gained significantly more on 1RM bench press and squat than high-stress participants. Social support was unrelated to gains.
- 검증 메모: The percentages (squat +27% vs +23%, bench +15% vs +12%) are not in the abstract and could not be verified from the full text. Cite only the direction.
- REF5 관련성: Same implication as Stults-Kolehmainen 2014: under REF5's pre-registered reading, slower windows could reflect life stress rather than the program. Supports adding a stress rating to the §23.1 audit.
- 조사 당시 URL: https://europepmc.org/article/med/18545186

### RC-17 · corrected

**Wilson JM, Marin PJ, Rhea MR, Wilson SM, Loenneke JP, Anderson JC. (2012) Concurrent training: a meta-analysis examining interference of aerobic and resistance exercises. J Strength Cond Res 26(8):2293-2307**

- meta-analysis · 2012 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22002517/
- 조사 요약: 21 studies. Interference with strength and hypertrophy depended on the endurance modality (running yes, cycling much less), frequency and duration, and was largely local to the trained body part (mostly lower body).
- 검증 결과: 21 studies, 422 ESs. Strength ES: strength-only 1.76, concurrent 1.44, endurance 0.78. Hypertrophy: 1.23, 0.85, 0.27. Power: 0.91, 0.55, 0.11. Running, but not cycling, significantly reduced hypertrophy and strength in concurrent training. Endurance frequency (r -0.26 to -0.35) and duration (r -0.29 to -0.75) correlated negatively with outcomes.
- 검증 메모: The abstract says nothing about interference being 'largely local to the trained body part (mostly lower body)'. That is an interpretation (running's eccentric load on the legs), not a reported finding. Note also that Schumann 2022 later found no running-vs-cycling moderator.
- REF5 관련성: Climbing is an upper-body, intermittent, grip-dominated activity, so the classic concurrent-training interference evidence is largely irrelevant to SQ and DL progression. The local concern is PULL, OAP and elbow load. This supports §3.2 (the engine ignores climbing) for the lower-body lifts.
- 조사 당시 표기: Wilson JM, Marin PJ, Rhea MR, et al. (2012) Concurrent training: a meta-analysis examining interference of aerobic and resistance exercises. J Strength Cond Res 26(8):2293-2307

### RC-18 · confirmed

## REF5 함의: 검증 후 유지

- §3.1 SQ always first — SUPPORTED (direct). Nunes JP 2021 (11 studies: multi-joint strength favoured MJ-first, ES 0.32; 'gains largest for exercises performed at the beginning') and the ACSM 2026 overview of 137 reviews both confirm it.
- §9 SQ gate rule 1 (≥48 h between hard SQ starts) — SUPPORTED as adequate for non-failure, low-volume work. Raastad 2000: back to baseline by 33 h after 3RM-load squatting in strength athletes. Morán-Navarro 2017: non-failure 3×5 and 6×5 squat/bench recovered significantly faster between 24 and 48 h than to-failure 3×10. Thomas 2018: only the much larger 10×5 at 80% needed about 72 h, with voluntary activation and twitch depressed for 48 h. Caveat: Belcher 2019 shows squat velocity still -8.6% at 72 h after 4 sets to failure at 80%. '48 h is ample' therefore depends on REF5 H3/H2 staying well short of failure.
- §9 SQ gate rule 2 (<2 hard starts per 168 h) — evidence SILENT, correctly characterised. No verified source tests a weekly cap at this volume. Reporting the ~24-25 days per SQ window as a cost rather than a recovery requirement remains fair.
- §22.2 no scheduled deload — SUPPORTED as weak evidence plus coaching consensus. Coleman 2024 found full cessation only weakly worse for lower-body strength (posterior 0.85-0.92, CrIs crossing zero) and no different for hypertrophy, power or endurance. Bell 2023 (Delphi) confirms deloads are expert practice, 'under-researched', and may be integrated autoregulatorily. The evidence does not meet a 'strong evidence' bar for a §26 proposal.
- §11 trigger 1 MICRO as a reactive valve — SUPPORTED at the level of coaching consensus. Bell 2023 defines a deload as reduced training stress, unanimously by reducing volume, and allows autoregulated deloads. A single reduced session is a micro version of this. Roth 2022 (evidence explicitly insufficient) tentatively favours not cutting volume habitually during a deficit.
- §20 / §23.1 recomposition at 0.25%/wk with strength progression — SUPPORTED. Murphy & Koehler 2022: strength gains not impaired by deficits (ES -0.31, p=0.28; within-group 0.84 vs 0.81), and lean-mass gain prevented at about 500 kcal/d. A ~200 kcal/d deficit (0.25%/wk at 75 kg ≈ 0.19 kg/wk) is well below that. Garthe 2011's slower arm (0.7%/wk achieved) gained 2.1% LBM while the faster arm did not. The arithmetic (~27 weeks to lose 5 kg) is correct.
- §23.1 protein criterion — PARTIALLY SUPPORTED, as stated. 1.6 g/kg is an energy-balance plateau (Morton 2018: 1.62 g/kg/d; Nunes EA 2022: effects at ≥1.6 g/kg/d in adults under 65). In a deficit, Helms 2014 (2.3-3.1 g/kg FFM) and Refalo/Trexler/Helms (29 studies, >97% probability of a linear dose-response per kg FFM, exploratory) point higher. It is correctly framed as a personal-target or audit note, not a protocol change.
- §23.1 adding a psychological-stress rating to the audit — SUPPORTED (cohort evidence). Stults-Kolehmainen 2014 (n=31): life-event stress moderated 96 h recovery of isometric force, energy, fatigue and soreness; perceived stress moderated force and energy. Bartholomew 2008 (n=135, 12 wk): low-stress lifters gained significantly more on squat and bench. This is an operational audit change only.
- Sleep as a plausible variance source for HOLD/FAIL — SUPPORTED in direction. Knowles 2018: consecutive nights of restriction reduce multi-joint (not single-joint) force. Reilly & Piercy 1994: 3 nights of 3 h sleep reduced maximal bench, leg press and deadlift and all submaximal 20-rep efforts from night 2. Lamon 2021: one night of total deprivation lowered MPS by 18%. Easow 2025: mixed results after a single night, with acute and chronic deprivation reducing some strength, power and endurance measures. Reading sleep as a 4-week pattern rather than single nights is well supported.
- Not reclassifying sleep-affected sets as INVALID — CONSISTENT with the user's fixed criteria and not contradicted by any source.
- §3.2 engine ignores climbing for SQ/DL/BP — SUPPORTED (extrapolation). Schumann 2022 (43 studies): concurrent aerobic training did not blunt maximal strength (SMD -0.06) or hypertrophy (-0.01). Petré 2021: in trained lifters, lower-body interference was a same-session effect (-0.66) and not seen with different sessions (-0.10, ns). No source is climbing-specific.
- The climbing risk is local upper-limb overuse — SUPPORTED. Schöffl 2015 (911 injuries: fingers 52%, shoulder 17.2%, forearm/elbow 9.1%, 58% overstrain, more overstrain in older and experienced climbers). Lutter 2020 (77.1% upper extremity, 56.1% chronic, bouldering 60.4% of acute). Quarmby 2023 (intensity, bouldering, crimping and previous injury are risk factors; volume conflicting). Handling it with the manual rules in §19.4/§19.5 rather than kg changes is defensible.
- §19.4 / §19.5(a) climbing spacing rules targeted at hard sessions and bouldering — SUPPORTED as reasonable, with limited direct evidence. Gáspari 2024: grip recovered by 24 h; tiredness normalised by 48 h and readiness by 60 h after a bouldering championship (n=9 elite). Quarmby 2023: intensity and bouldering, not volume, are the stronger risk factors.
- §19.5(c) two-week OAP rung minimum is a minimum dwell, not a tendon-adaptation window — STILL SUPPORTED. Bohm 2015 shows tendon stiffness adapts to high (>70% MVC) loading (SMD 0.90). Every included intervention lasted at least about 8 weeks, so no data support 2 weeks as an 'adaptation window'. Quarmby 2023 confirms previous injury as the strongest risk factor, which argues for conservatism with prior elbow complaints.
- §7.5 OAP to slot 2 as a single §26-style PROPOSAL (not a directive) — STILL SUPPORTED as a weak, indirect option. Exercise-order evidence is confirmed (Nunes JP 2021; ACSM 2026: strength enhanced when the exercise is placed at the beginning of the session). Branscheidt 2019 confirms that practising under fatigue impairs later skill learning, partly centrally. The stated costs to BP and the one-change-at-a-time framing remain appropriate.
- §19.4 no kg cut after short gaps — SUPPORTED. Bosquet 2013 (103 studies): cessation losses are dose-dependent with duration and smaller in recreationally trained than inactive people, so an ~8-day gap costs little maximal strength.
- Cost of the 5-lift, no-accessory principle under a deficit (description only) — SUPPORTED. ACSM 2026 associates hypertrophy with ≥10 sets/wk, and Murphy & Koehler 2022 confirm lean-mass gains are attenuated in a deficit.
- Spec line 302 '48시간 분리는 구조적으로 보장된다' (48 h separation is structurally guaranteed) — I confirmed the spec contains this sentence (docs/ref5-program-spec.md:302; also docs/ref5-v1.4-oap-proposal.md:34). Logically, focus alternation guarantees different sessions, not 48 clock-hours, under an irregular schedule. This is a logic/log point, not a literature claim. I did not verify the user's log, and no source on weighted-pull-up→OAP recovery was found, so 'evidence SILENT' is accurate.

## REF5 함의: 검증에서 약화

- Spec correction for §22.2 ('50 trained participants' should be 39) — WEAKENED / mis-aimed. Coleman 2024's Methods state 50 resistance-trained volunteers were recruited and block-randomised, and 39 completed. Only the abstract says '39 randomly assigned'. The spec's 50 is defensible as enrolment; at most add '50 enrolled, 39 analysed'. The correction that IS warranted is to spec line 842's '하체 근력 향상이 열위였고' (lower-body strength gains were inferior). The paper shows only a weak probabilistic tendency favouring continuous training (squat 1RM -3.6 kg, CrI -10.4 to +3.2, posterior 0.85; isometric posterior 0.92), with credible intervals crossing zero, so 'inferior' overstates it.
- Sleep effect size 'about -7.6% performance with ≤6 h sleep (Craven 2022)' — WEAKENED for REF5's lifts. -7.56% is the all-task mean and is driven by endurance and other tasks. Craven's STRENGTH-specific effect was -2.85%, significant only after TOTAL sleep deprivation; upper-body strength was not affected and morning tasks were largely unaffected. The operational suggestion to choose MICRO after a single night under ~6 h is therefore weakly supported. The stronger basis is accumulated short sleep over consecutive nights (Knowles 2018; Reilly & Piercy 1994: 3 nights of 3 h). Reframe as: consider MICRO after several consecutive short nights, or after a near-sleepless night.
- Reilly & Piercy 1994 as evidence that REF5's judged format (fixed submaximal load × reps) is 'the most sleep-sensitive' — WEAKENED. The submaximal task was the load lifted for 20 reps at a fixed perceived-exertion rating, not reps at a fixed load. The abstract does not say submaximal declined more than maximal (only that it declined in all 4 tasks, including the curl, where maximal did not). Its mapping onto H3/H2/V and BP 2×5 outcomes is indirect.
- Bartolomei 2017 'heavy 8×3 never depressed maximal contraction' as support that the 168 h cap is stricter than recovery requires — WEAKENED. The abstract shows HI caused a smaller acute MVIC drop than HV (not no drop) and no difference from baseline at 72 h. The '~11% at 72 h' for HV is unverified. The broader conclusion (heavy low-rep work recovers faster than high-volume work) still holds, but the 'never below baseline' wording should be dropped.
- Belcher 2019 cited for 'authors suggest ≥48 h between sessions on the same lift' — WEAKENED. That recommendation is not in the abstract. The authors' stated conclusion is that recovery time course is similar across lifts and that the deadlift needs no longer recovery. The squat velocity deficit lasting to 72 h after to-failure sets slightly cuts against calling 48 h 'ample' unless REF5 sets stay well short of failure.
- Concurrent-training interference 'largely local to the trained body part' (Wilson 2012) — WEAKENED as a cited finding. Wilson's abstract reports modality (running, not cycling), frequency and duration moderators, but says nothing about local specificity; that is an interpretation. Schumann 2022 found no running-vs-cycling moderator at all. The conclusion that climbing does not interfere with SQ/DL still stands on Schumann and Petré (minimal interference, same-session-dependent), but remains an extrapolation to climbing.
- Bohm 2015 'interventions ≥12 weeks produced larger effects' as grounds that tendon remodelling runs on months — WEAKENED. The duration subgroup difference was not significant (SMD 0.91 for ≥12 wk vs 0.81 for 8-12 wk, p=0.7). The point that 2 weeks is untested and should not be called a full adaptation window stands, because no included study was that short. The 'months' framing should not be attributed to Bohm's duration moderator.
- Protein target '≈2.0-2.2 g/kg BODY WEIGHT' from Refalo/Trexler/Helms — WEAKENED in precision. The dose-response was credible per kg FFM (β 0.06, HDI 0.01-0.12) but NOT per kg body mass (β 0.07, HDI -0.01 to 0.14). The authors label the findings exploratory. Helms 2014's range is also per kg FFM (2.3-3.1). The higher-than-1.6 direction is supported, but a specific g/kg BW number is an extrapolation. The citation should also read 'An Updated Systematic Review With Meta-Regression', first published online January 2025; the 2026 48(4):398-412 issue details are unverified.
- Murphy & Koehler 2022 regression specifics (slope -0.31 ES per 1000 kcal/d, intercept 0.16, strength k≈5) — UNVERIFIED. Only the ~500 kcal/d threshold and the abstract ESs could be confirmed. The qualitative inference (a ~200 kcal/d deficit attenuates but does not abolish lean-mass gain) is reasonable, but the numeric slope and intercept should not be quoted as verified.
- Garthe 2011 per-lift 1RM claims (the slow arm improved all lifts; the fast arm only squat) — UNVERIFIED. The abstract gives no per-lift 1RM results. Only the rates and LBM results are confirmed. The slow arm's achieved rate also had a large SD (0.7 ± 0.8%/wk), and both arms lost about 5.5% of body weight over different durations.
- Lamon 2021 testosterone '-24% in men' — CORRECTED. The sample was mixed-sex (7M/6F) and the 24% AUC decrease is pooled. It was one night of TOTAL deprivation, so applying it to everyday short sleep is an extrapolation.
- Gáspari 2024 as the climbing recovery time-course anchor — WEAKENED in weight. n=9 (4 men, 5 women) after a single competition; the 48-60 h readiness figure comes from perceptual markers with larger individual variability. The supporting role is fine, but n=9 should be stated.
- Bartholomew 2008 percentages (squat ~+27% vs +23%, bench ~+15% vs +12%) — UNVERIFIED. The abstract reports only the significant direction. The implication (add a stress rating to the audit) is unaffected.
- Barakat 2020 specifics (protein 2.6-3.5 g/kg FFM, ≥3 sessions/wk) — UNVERIFIED. The paper exists, but the abstract has no numbers. It remains review-level support only.
- Branscheidt 2019 figures (~68% skill level, ~2× time to catch up) — UNVERIFIED, and the mechanism is described as 'partly' central. The slot-2 proposal's reliance on it should stay at 'weak, specific, extrapolated', as already stated.
- Bell 2023 citation — the title conflated two papers. PMID 37730925 is 'Integrating Deloading into Strength and Physique Sports Training Programmes: An International Delphi Consensus Approach' (Bell, Strafford, Coleman, Androulakis Korakakis, Nolan). The substance of the implication is unaffected.
- Quarmby 2023 co-authors were wrong (Zhang M, Geisler M, Javorsky T, Mugele H, Cassel M, Lawley J, not 'Zhang J, Engel T'). The substance of the implication is unaffected.

## REF5 함의: 조사 단계 원안

- §3.1 SQ always first — SUPPORTED (direct). Nunes JP 2021 and the ACSM 2026 position stand both find that strength gains are larger for the lift done at the start of the session. No change.
- §9 SQ hard-density gate, rule 1 (≥48 h since the last hard SQ start) — SUPPORTED, and on the conservative side (direct plus some extrapolation). Raastad 2000 recovered by about 33 h after a much harsher 3RM session. Morán-Navarro 2017 found non-failure 3×5 work recovered between 24 and 48 h. Bartolomei 2017 found heavy 8×3 work never depressed maximal contraction. Thomas 2018 and Belcher 2019 suggest 48-72 h only after high-volume or to-failure squat work. REF5 H3/H2 sessions are low volume and non-failure, so 48 h is ample.
- §9 SQ gate, rule 2 (fewer than 2 hard starts in any open 168 h window) — evidence SILENT. No study tests a weekly cap on hard sessions at this volume. The recovery data above do not require it. The observed ~24-25 days to fill one SQ window of 6 is a cost of this rule plus the irregular schedule, not something recovery physiology demands. Under the fixed principles this is reported as a cost, not a recommendation. It would be a candidate only if the user's post-audit data showed recovery headroom.
- §11 trigger 1, MICRO 'for any reason' as the self-recovery valve — SUPPORTED in principle (consensus plus extrapolation). Coleman 2024 and Bosquet 2013 show that full cessation costs some strength. Roth 2022 suggests volume should not be cut habitually during a deficit. A reduced session rather than a skipped one fits the Bell 2023 Delphi idea of a 'reactive deload'. Operational suggestion, no protocol change: choose MICRO when sleep the night before was under about 6 h or on a high-stress day. This uses the existing valve. Craven 2022, Knowles 2018 and Reilly & Piercy 1994 show multi-rep submaximal compound sets, the REF5 judgment format, are the most sleep-sensitive. Do NOT reclassify sleep-affected sets as INVALID; that would loosen the judgment criteria, which the user has fixed.
- §22.2 no scheduled deload — SUPPORTED (weak direct evidence, coaching consensus). The evidence for planned deloads is not strong enough to meet the user's 'strong evidence' bar for a §26 proposal. Coleman 2024's posterior favoured continuous training only weakly. Spec correction: §22.2 says the trial had '훈련경험자 50명' (50 trained participants); the PeerJ paper reports 39 randomised (29 men, 10 women).
- §20 / §23.1 recomposition at 0.25%/wk while expecting strength progression — SUPPORTED (direct for strength, extrapolation for lean mass). 0.25%/wk at 75 kg is about 0.19 kg/wk, or roughly a 200 kcal/d deficit. Murphy & Koehler 2022 found strength gains unimpaired in deficits; their regression puts lean-mass gain at ~0 near 500 kcal/d, so at ~200 kcal/d it should be attenuated, not eliminated. Garthe 2011's slow arm (0.7%/wk) still gained strength and lean mass. The §20 stop rule (end the cut on SQ stagnation or an immediate decrease) matches the evidence. Cost to note: long-run SQ (G1, 175 kg) depends partly on lean mass. A long cut with attenuated hypertrophy is a real trade-off, and losing 5 kg at 0.25%/wk takes about 27 weeks.
- §23.1 recovery audit, protein criterion (days below 1.6 g/kg) — PARTIALLY SUPPORTED. 1.6 g/kg is the plateau measured mostly in energy balance (Morton 2018, Nunes 2022). For lean, trained people in a deficit, Helms 2014 and Refalo/Trexler/Helms 2026 point to a higher, dose-dependent need. While the cut is active, a target of about 2.0-2.2 g/kg BW (≈150-165 g/d at 75 kg) is better supported. This is a personal nutrition setting or an audit-threshold note, not a protocol change, so it does not use up the 'one protocol change at a time' budget.
- §23.1 recovery audit, loss-rate criterion (more than 0.25%/wk) — currently UNVERIFIABLE (measurement gap, not an evidence question). Bodyweight is logged as 75 kg at every session, which looks like a default. With that log, the loss-rate audit cannot run, and any total load that depends on bodyweight (PULL) is unchecked. Operational fix: log measured morning bodyweight several times a week and use the 7-day average §20 already refers to. Without it, the first branch of the pre-registered reading (recovery first) cannot be tested.
- §23.1 recovery audit, scope — evidence suggests ADDING psychological stress (and alcohol, if relevant) alongside sleep, protein and loss rate. Stults-Kolehmainen 2014 found higher chronic stress slowed 96 h force recovery; Bartholomew 2008 found high-stress lifters gained less over 12 weeks. A simple daily 1-5 stress rating would let a slow window be traced to stress rather than to the program. This is an operational audit change; the engine and judgment criteria stay as they are.
- User's self-reported poor recovery — evidence implies this is the most plausible single source of HOLD/FAIL if they appear. Sleep is the strongest documented effect: about -7.6% performance with ≤6 h sleep (Craven 2022), and multi-joint force falls after consecutive short nights (Knowles 2018). Sleep loss also lowers muscle protein synthesis (Lamon 2021). The combination of a deficit, possible short sleep and stress compounds. This agrees with the user's pre-registered reading that a low success rate is first insufficient recovery. So far every window has ended INCREASE, which suggests loads are still below what recovery limits. Recovery variance will matter more as loads approach true capacity.
- §3.2 the engine ignores climbing — SUPPORTED for SQ, DL and BP; evidence SILENT on PULL and OAP. Concurrent-training meta-analyses (Wilson 2012, Schumann 2022, Petré 2021) find interference mostly local to the trained body part and mostly a same-session effect, for aerobic modes. Climbing does not plausibly interfere with lower-body lifts. The relevant risk is local upper-limb overuse: fingers, elbow and shoulder (Schöffl 2015, Lutter 2020, Quarmby 2023). That is better handled by the manual rules in §19.4 and §19.5 than by changing kg, so §3.2 is defensible.
- §19.4 (no heavy climbing within 48 h before PULL focus) and §19.5(a) (no climbing the day before or the day of OAP) — SUPPORTED as reasonable (limited direct evidence). Gáspari 2024: after hard bouldering, grip recovered by 24 h but tiredness and readiness took 48-60 h. Quarmby 2023: intensity and bouldering, not climbing volume, are the stronger overuse risk factors. So the rules should target hard sessions and bouldering specifically; easy climbing is less of a concern. This is a refinement for the user's operating guidance only.
- Spec line 302 claim that the PULL-focus → OAP separation is '48시간 분리는 구조적으로 보장된다' (48 h separation is structurally guaranteed) — CONTRADICTED by the user's own log. Heavy PULL and OAP happened about 24 h apart twice. Focus alternation guarantees different sessions, not 48 h of clock time, under an irregular schedule. The evidence on recovery between weighted pull-ups and OAP is SILENT (no studies found). Before any change: audit OAP outcomes against hours since the last PULL focus. A user-chosen MICRO on that day is not a fix, because MICRO does not apply to the OAP slot. The honest options are scheduling discipline or, if the audit shows an effect, a §26-style proposal for an engine gate. That would count as the one protocol change.
- §19.5(c) two-week minimum per OAP rung as a 'tendon adaptation window' — SUPPORTED as a minimum but MISLABELLED as a full adaptation window (extrapolation). Bohm 2015 shows tendon stiffness adapts to high loads, with larger effects at ≥12 weeks. Two weeks gives some time for connective tissue, not completed remodelling. Previous elbow complaints should make the user more conservative here (Quarmby 2023: previous injury is the strongest risk factor).
- §7.5 OAP slot position (slot 3: SQ → BP focus → OAP → OHP) — single protocol candidate, presented as a §26-style PROPOSAL, not a directive (extrapolation). Move OAP to slot 2 (SQ → OAP → BP focus → OHP). Basis: G6 ranks OAP as the top upper-body goal. Nunes 2021 and ACSM 2026 find exercises done earlier gain more. Branscheidt 2019 shows skill practice under fatigue impairs learning that persists. Costs: BP focus moves later and may progress more slowly, and BP is judged so its HOLD rate could change; OAP grip and elbow fatigue could also carry into BP. Run it as the only change, observing at least 2 BP windows and at least 2 rungs. The evidence is indirect, so it is an option, not a requirement.
- §19.4 return after a gap (8 days before 10/14) — SUPPORTED. Bosquet 2013 shows strength loss after short cessation is small and dose-dependent. Not cutting kg after short gaps is consistent with this, and short gaps act as unplanned deloads, which further weakens the case for scheduled ones.
- Cost of the fixed 5-lift, no-accessory principle under a deficit (description only, no recommendation) — ACSM 2026 associates hypertrophy with ≥10 sets/wk per muscle. REF5's weekly sets per muscle group are probably below that for several groups, and the cut further attenuates lean-mass accrual (Murphy 2022). Strength in REF5's 3-5 rep range can progress for a while through neural and skill adaptation, but long-run SQ and BP targets will eventually need hypertrophy. This is a structural cost the user has accepted.

## 불확실성

- Most studies on recovery between sessions (Raastad, Morán-Navarro, Bartolomei, Thomas, Belcher) use lower-body tests, untrained or moderately trained young men, and single sessions. None tests REF5's exact H3/H2/V loads over many weeks.
- No studies were found on how weighted pull-ups or one-arm pull-ups recover over time, or on how climbing interacts with heavy pulling. The PULL-focus → OAP spacing and the OAP climbing rules rest on extrapolation and coaching opinion.
- Gáspari 2024, the only climbing recovery time-course found, used elite boulderers after a competition. The user's climbing (frequency and intensity unclear; possibly 1-2 times a month or more) may recover faster or slower.
- Branscheidt 2019 used a lab finger-force task in non-athletes. Its relevance to OAP sequencing is speculative, which is why the slot-2 idea is only a proposal.
- Murphy & Koehler 2022's strength result rests on few studies (k about 5). The ~500 kcal/d threshold for losing lean-mass gain is a regression estimate, not a measured cut-off. The 0.25%/wk to ~200 kcal/d conversion assumes typical tissue energy density.
- Refalo/Trexler/Helms 2026 is a Bayesian exploratory meta-regression; the authors label the protein dose-response as exploratory.
- Sleep studies mostly use acute deprivation or restriction protocols, not real-world chronic short sleep in trained lifters. Effect sizes for the user's actual sleep pattern are unknown.
- The user's inputs that matter here are not measured: sleep duration, protein intake, stress, alcohol and true bodyweight (logged as a constant 75 kg). Until they are, the §23.1 recovery audit cannot separate recovery from stimulus as causes of any future HOLD or FAIL.
- Knee pain, if present, could affect SQ results independently of recovery or programming. This dimension did not assess it.
- Schöffl 2015's PMID was inferred from a secondary citation and its abstract seen only on a mirror. Belcher 2019 and Bell 2023 titles were not re-verified word for word. Bosquet 2013 was accessed only by DOI. The Reilly & Piercy 1994 URL is a database record, not PubMed.
- Every window so far ended INCREASE, so there is no internal data yet on how recovery variables affect REF5 outcomes. All recovery-related implications are forward-looking.
